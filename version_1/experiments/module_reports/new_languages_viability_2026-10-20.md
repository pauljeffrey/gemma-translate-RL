# Can more languages join the RL? Data audit, zero-shot probe, confidence-weighted reward, plan (D-47, 2026-10-20)

Owner question (2026-10-20): can we ADD languages (Fula, Ga, Kanuri, Tamazight, Dinka, Chokwe, Tamasheq, N'Ko, the SMOL-only languages, languages with no human reference at all) to the RL with the English prompts and the reward machinery we already have, and how far can each be handled?

Everything below was **measured on CPU** (old box, `nice`d, 20 cores) or read from the Hub's metadata; nothing was pushed (Hub, bucket), nothing in the running trainer, the live yaml, `languages.py`, the calibration files or the prompt directories was touched. Raw files, scripts and tests are listed in section 9. Numbers that are estimates or recollection are labelled so.

## 0. Summary (the answers)

1. **No new language can join the current RL run now, and RL cannot teach one.** With the plain ISO tag forced into the answer (the most favourable condition), the step-925 phase-2c policy writes **0.0-0.4 % right-language text (GlotLID) in 14 of 16 new languages and 2.1 % (nde) / 4.6 % (alz) in the other two**, at T = 1 over 240 samples each (60 sentences x 4 samples), against 98-100 % for 15 trained yardstick languages. It writes the language of its nearest neighbours instead: Fula comes out as Bambara (48 % of greedy outputs) or Dyula (8 %), Dinka as Luba-Kasai, Kanuri as Kinyarwanda, Mauritian Creole as Setswana/Sesotho. Asked for a new language by name only, it writes the tag of an unrelated trained language (`lua`/`lun` for Fula) or no tag. Under the present reward every such sample is `wrong_language` or `bad_prefix` (-2): the group is flat, the advantage is 0 and nothing is learned (and nothing is damaged).
2. **The one language that is already partly handled is North Ndebele (nde):** the model writes Zulu/South-Ndebele-like text (GlotLID: `nbl` 82 %, `zul` 12 %, `nde` 3 %) that scores chrF++ **27.6** on the 60 NTREX sentences (floor of unrelated text 14.3) and 29.4 on SmolSent; asking for **Zulu** scores **33.9** against the same North-Ndebele reference. It is Nguni, not North Ndebele, and neither GlotLID (recall on human North Ndebele only 0.74-0.76: 19 % are called `zul`) nor the QE (AUC 0.89, valid) can tell the difference. Adding it to RL would add nothing.
3. **The worry about "consensus among junk" is confirmed and measured.** Among the junk samples (no LID guard) the consensus still correlates with chrF++ against the human reference within a prompt (rho 0.23), but 0.18 of that is also obtained against the chrF++ of an UNRELATED reference sentence (length, punctuation, numbers, names copied from the source); the content-only part is **0.04 (se 0.02)** against **0.26 (se 0.02)** for the trained languages (and 0.25 for the same languages at an early SFT checkpoint). The sample with the highest consensus is more often in the dominant WRONG language than an average sample (mfe 36 -> 47 %, dik 35 -> 42 %, gaa 16 -> 27 %, alz 7 -> 20 %, nqo 18 -> 31 %), and 4-18 % longer. What protects the current run is the GlotLID guard, not the consensus design; it does so only where GlotLID can identify the language.
4. **Reference-free confidence works as a gate and does not (yet) work as a graded quality estimate.** GlotLID pass rate, valid-sample rate and mean token log-prob separate all 15 trained languages from all 15 non-writable ones with margins (LID 0.98 vs <= 0.05; valid rate 0.96 vs <= 0.05; log-prob only 0.2 nats). Across the 31 languages the consensus level (rho 0.85) and the mean log-prob (0.81) rank the languages by quality; among the 30 (language, checkpoint) rows of trained languages the consensus level keeps rho 0.71 and the log-prob 0.61. The linear model that turns the features into a chrF++ estimate is accurate inside the trained languages (leave-one-language-out MAE 3.5 chrF++ against an SD of 8.0) but **over-predicts the non-writable languages by 6 chrF++ on average** (predicted 10.2, actual 4.2): do not use it to set weights; use the weakest-link rule of section 4.4.
5. **Data exists for most of them, a metric for few.** Of 25 candidate languages, 16 have a human English-referenced set (FLORES+ / SmolSent / NTREX), 16 have >= 5,000 English-parallel rows somewhere on the Hub (human, mined or religious), 10 have a GlotLID recall >= 0.90 on human text, 9 are named on the AfriCOMET card, and **only 6 have QE that separates human from mismatched references at AUC >= 0.70 on our 150-pair sets (fuv 0.70, gaa 0.70, knc 0.73, mfe 0.72, dov 0.72, nde 0.89)**; for tzm, nqo, taq-Tifinagh, sus, tig, bci, alz the QE is at 0.50-0.60 (useless). Six languages (fuv, knc, tzm, dik, taq, nqo) have the 6,193-sentence human NLLB-Seed set; six more (mnk, snk, srr, mas, kln, mer) have no human reference at all and cannot be validated.
6. **Plan.** (a) Now: nothing in the run; optionally put the confidence gate + the relative log-prob trust (section 4) in front of any consensus-only language. (b) Fold the seven viable Latin-script languages (fuv, dik, taq, knc, gaa, mfe, cjk; then bci, dje, alz, dov, sus) into the **SFT of the inter-African model (next month, new reference model)** or a dedicated top-up SFT of ~50M tokens followed by a short b64/b256 RL phase; estimate 6-12 GPU-h plus data engineering. (c) Not viable: tig (Ge'ez script, GlotLID blind, 4 k software strings), tzm / taq-Tifinagh / nqo (scripts the model cannot write at all, 4-11x the tokens, QE useless) until a script experiment succeeds, and the six languages without any reference. A **measured** CPU micro top-up (3,100 human Fula pairs, 1 epoch, 37 CPU-minutes, section 7): the model writes the `ful` tag 100 % and GlotLID-Fula 98 %, but chrF++ stays at 13.2 (floor 8.8, loops in 15 %), trained languages lose 2.5-8 chrF++; and the post-top-up model is exactly the dangerous case: GlotLID 100 %, valid 97 %, gate open, yet consensus content-rho -0.04 and log-prob -4.0, which only the log-prob arm of the trust rule catches (trust 0).
7. **The owner's idea (stop after b1024, add languages, continue through b2048 + mixed) would not work as intended:** the languages would see ~100-250 prompts each (1.7-4 k samples), only on the longest windows (b2048 / mixed; the short bins are behind us), with no way to write their tag, and the KL reference of the phase is the phase start. Section 6.

## 1. What was run

* **Policy under test.** `<HF_ORG_LEGACY>/gemma-3-270m-translate-rl2c`, branch `latest` (training_state: **step 925**, pushed 2026-10-20 20:20 UTC, phase 2c b512/b1024 stage), fp32, HF `generate` on CPU. Baseline: `google/gemma-3-270m-it`. For the graded-capability validation also the SFT checkpoint **step 2,250** (`<HF_ORG_LEGACY>/gemma3-270m-translate`, 0.13 epoch-equivalents, val macro loss 1.99; cached on the box).
* **Prompt.** The repo's own `format.py` (`Translate the text to {name}:  {text}`, Gemma chat template, generation prompt `<start_of_turn>model\n`), T = 1.0, top_p 1, top_k 0 for samples (the RL sampling of D-39), greedy as the 5th group member (the RL group has the greedy completion appended), `max_new_tokens` 160 (FLORES / NTREX sets) or 128 (SmolSent / local sets). A group = greedy + 4 samples per sentence (RL uses 16; consensus needs >= 3 valid members).
* **Conditions for a NEW language.** `prefill`: the instruction names the language ("Translate the text to Fula:") and the **plain ISO tag is appended to the prompt** (`fuv `): the model only has to write the translation (this is the main row; the real RL would need exactly this because the model never writes a new tag). `free`: no prefill, the model writes its own tag. `ctrl:<relative>`: the instruction names the closest TRAINED relative (wol, hau for Fula ...) and the output is scored against the new language's reference (does the model just write the relative, and how much chrF++ does that earn?). `base`: gemma-3-270m-it, same prompt.
* **Yardstick.** 15 languages we DO train, spread from strong to weak, with the production tag: swh hau wol bem sag nyn yor kea umb ibb efi ach kbp nus dyu (shi was probed too but its NTREX reference is in Arabic script while our `shi` is Latin: excluded; bin / wes have no human reference: not probed).
* **Reference sets (60 sentences each, English 5-40 words, seeded; 150-sentence versions for the QE validity test).** FLORES+ dev (997, CC-BY-SA-4.0; the dev split, which was used for the QE calibration and is not training data) for fuv, knc, zgh (Standard Moroccan Tamazight), tzm (FLORES-200 original), dik, cjk, taq, nqo, mfe and 11 yardsticks; SmolSent (863, CC-BY-4.0) for gaa, dje, sus, bci, tig, alz, dov and ach, efi; NTREX (1,997) for nde; SALT (nyn) and IbomMT (ibb). Secondary sets (SmolSent ff / fuc / din / kr / nd / ber ...) were run greedy-only.
* **Metrics.** chrF++ = sacrebleu `CHRF(word_order=2)`; **floor** = chrF++ of the same output against the reference of an unrelated sentence (unrelated Latin-script text of the right length scores 6-16: it depends on the language and the reference); **excess** = chrF++ - floor (the number to read). GlotLID v3 (`model_v3.bin`) with the accepted label set and minimum length of the production calibration (trained languages) or of `scripts/newlang_registry.py` (new languages, >= 30 characters). `valid` = the reward's hard checks (tag, finished, non-empty, not a copy, not a loop, length ratio, GlotLID). Consensus = `rl/consensus.py` on the valid members. Mean log-prob = the sampler's own mean token log-prob of the answer (tag excluded). AfriCOMET-QE = the production QE model (`masakhane/africomet-qe-stl-1.1`), CPU, on the laptop.
* **Precision.** 60 sentences: the standard error of a language's mean chrF++ is 0.3-1.8 (median 0.7); of its excess 0.1-1.8 (median 0.6). Differences below ~2 chrF++ between two languages are noise.

## 4. Confidence-weighted reward (deliverable 3)

### 4.1 Where confidence has to act (read from `rl/reward.py`, `rl/consensus.py`, D-37 / D-38 / D-43)

A completion's hard checks (tag, finished, non-empty, not a copy, not a loop, length ratio, **GlotLID where it is on**) decide `valid`; consensus is the mean chrF++ against the OTHER valid completions and needs >= 3 valid ones, otherwise the group falls back to the rubric-only value 0; the language weight (calibration `reliability`, 0.5 for consensus-only languages) multiplies the advantage; the advantage is standardised per group. Three consequences for the design: (1) **a constant rescaling of rewards inside a group is removed by the standardisation**, so a "cap" can only act through ties at the top and through the weight; the weight and the gate are what control how much a language moves the policy; (2) the GlotLID guard is what removes junk today; it is switched off where the measured recall is < 0.90 (nde, dje, tig, dov, sus, alz), which are the languages where junk would be paid; (3) for a language that the model cannot write, valid samples do not exist, so every group is skipped and the language is silently dead weight in the draw.

### 4.3 What does NOT work (kept in the report on purpose)

* **A regression from the features to the quality does not extrapolate.** `excess ~ a + b1 consensus + b2 log-prob`, fit on the trained languages: leave-one-LANGUAGE-out Spearman 0.75, MAE 3.5 chrF++ (SD 8.0) inside the 30 trained rows, but on the 15 non-writable languages it predicts 10.2 on average against an actual 4.2 (MAE 6.0, Spearman 0.39; the fitted log-prob coefficient is negative because of collinearity). It would hand a positive weight to languages the model cannot write. The weight must be a rule on the separating features, not a regression on quality.
* **Absolute log-prob thresholds are not portable**: same 15 languages, median -1.14 at the RL step-925 policy and -2.91 at the SFT step-2,250 checkpoint. Used as a run-relative quantity (log-prob minus the median of the languages that currently work) the rule's AUC for "usable" (LID >= 0.8 and excess >= 5) over the 46 rows is 0.917 (absolute: 0.895), with 0 % of the usable rows given trust 0 (absolute: 19 %).
* **Consensus level cannot be the gate** (overlap), and **GlotLID cannot be the only gate** (blind for tig, dov under its own label, snk, kln; unusable for nde / dje / sus / alz).
* The middle zone of the rule (0.05 < GlotLID pass < 0.98, which only a partly trained language would occupy) is an interpolation: no language of the probe sits there except the early-checkpoint rows (nyn 0.87).

### 4.4 The rules (implemented, tested, NOT wired into `TranslationReward`)

`ConfidenceParams` defaults (set from the margins above): `min_valid 3`, `lid_pass_min 0.5`, `distinct_min 0.5`; trust ramps `lid 0.55 -> 0.90`, log-prob `-3.1 -> -2.4` absolute or `-2.0 -> -1.3` relative to the median log-prob of the working languages, valid fraction `0.10 -> 0.50`; trust = the weakest of the three ramps, 0 below 0.15.

1. **Gate (a group contributes no gradient):** fewer than 3 valid samples, or GlotLID pass of the checked samples < 0.5, or the valid samples are copies of each other (distinct < 0.5). Applied per group; costs nothing for the 15 trained languages (97-100 % pass), removes 100 % of the groups of the non-writable ones.
2. **Advantage weight = language weight (as now) x trust:** trust from the running features of the language (EMA over groups, plain mean for the first 20). Measured over the 46 rows: mean trust 0.98 for usable rows (relative form), 0.20 for the unusable ones; **all 16 new languages (nde included) have trust 0 at the RL checkpoint**; the 4 unusable rows with trust > 0 are early-SFT rows near the usability line; kea (lowest trained log-prob) gets 0.59. For a consensus-only language the product with the D-43 weight (0.5 when the content rho >= 0.25) is the new weight; the content rho of the trained languages is 0.10-0.37, so the D-43 rule stays.
3. **Cap on consensus-only reward:** `consensus_cap(trust)` = rubric-only value + trust x (top of range - rubric-only value): at trust 0 a completion can earn at most the rubric-only value 0, at trust 1 the full range. Because the group standardisation removes constants, the cap acts as ties at the top (a self-agreeing mode cannot out-score a more varied correct sibling by more than the cap allows) and, with the weight, as the amplitude. It is the least validated of the three rules: the data support the gate and the weight (they act on measured separations), the cap rests on the attractor measurement (modest: +4 to +13 points of dominant-wrong-language share in 8 of 15 languages).
4. **The rule for "consensus among junk":** pay NO consensus reward unless (i) GlotLID pass >= 0.55 (full at 0.90) at T = 1 over the language's running window, (ii) >= 3 valid samples in the group, (iii) distinctness >= 0.5, (iv) the language's relative log-prob is within 2.0 nats of the median of the working languages (full trust within 1.3). Where GlotLID has no usable label (tig, dov, snk, kln, nde, dje) condition (i) is replaced by the **modal-label share** of the group (>= 0.9 for working languages, 0.23-0.48 for junk) AND an external human-reference check on a held-out sample: such a language is not given consensus-only reward at all unless a reference-based dev measurement (the D-43 procedure) shows content rho >= 0.25.
5. **Wiring (not done):** `TranslationReward.__call__` already holds `status`, `lid_label`, `consensus_chrf`; it needs per-sample mean log-prob from the trainer (`completion_logprobs`), a `ConfidenceTracker` on the reward object, `RewardOutput.advantage_scale` per sample (0 for gated-out groups, trust x weight otherwise) consumed where `weights.append(... reliability ...)` is applied (reward.py line ~420), and `confidence/*` diagnostics. About 80 lines + tests; kept out of the live code tree on purpose.

* **Evidence for the data amount (measured on the yardsticks):** languages trained on 4.0-12 k unique English sentences (kea 4,006, ibb 4,851, kbp 7,045, dyu 11.9 k) are written with 98-100 % GlotLID and excess 5-10 chrF++ by the RL policy, and at the 2,250-step SFT checkpoint (0.13 epoch) every one of the 15 yardsticks already had GlotLID 87-100 %. So **~5-10 k human pairs per language is a plausible lower bound for "writable"**, not a guarantee; section 7 measures it once for Fula.
* **Data per language (clean-pair target after LID + QE gating + de-duplication; tokens at ~105 per pair incl. template for Latin script, measured fertility 1.65-2.0 Gemma tokens per English token):** fuv 100 k (Seed 6.2 k, mined slice; Pontoon / Weblate strings excluded) = 10 M tokens; dik 60 k = 6 M; taq(Latin) 60 k = 6 M; knc 7 k (x3 passes) = 2 M; gaa 100 k (JW) = 10 M; mfe 100 k = 10 M; cjk 36 k (x2) = 7 M. **Tier 1 total ~460 k pairs ~ 50 M tokens (2.3 % of the original 2.2 B-token SFT).** Tier 2 (bci 80 k, dje 60 k, alz 7.5 k, dov 8 k, sus 100 k): ~25 M tokens more.
* **Licences.** Seed CC-BY-SA-4.0 (share-alike), FLORES / NTREX CC-BY-SA (eval only), SMOL CC-BY-4.0 (eval only), JW300-derived sets CC-BY-4.0 on the card but jw.org copyright upstream, NLLB-mined sets: none declared, upstream NC (the open decision of D-46), Bible per-row CC, Farmer-QA NC (excluded), YouVersion-derived Susu: "other". Same classes and caveats as the existing build (`licence` / `source_dataset` per row).
* **How to mix without forgetting.** Do NOT patch the RL policy: its KL reference is the phase start, so new-language tokens would show up as large k3 KL (D-45 outlier mechanism, guard at mean KL 0.15). Instead (a) put the new languages in the **next SFT** (the inter-African build is a new SFT with a new reference model: add the new-language pairs to `build_afri2afri_sft.py`'s language list; the mined fulah-X / dinka-X pairs also fill inter-African directions), or (b) a dedicated top-up SFT starting from the SFT final model with 50 % replay of the original UniMax windows (alpha-weighted), lr <= 1e-5 constant, 1-2 passes of the new data, a macro dev chrF++ check on the 77 languages before/after (gate: <= 0.3 macro chrF++ loss; else interpolate weights between pre and post), then **a new RL phase 3 with the top-up model as reference**, starting at b64 / b256 only for the new languages.
* **GPU time (estimates).** SFT: the original run measured 22 s per 131,072-token step (6 k tokens/s) on its box; 50 M tokens of new data + 1:1 replay = 760 steps = 4.6 h at that rate; the RTX PRO 6000 should be 2-4x faster (not measured): **1.5-5 GPU-h**. QE / LID gating of ~2 M mined pairs: ~1 GPU-h (CPU would be ~70 h). Short RL phase: 7 languages x 3,000 prompts x 16 = 336 k completions at b64 / b256 (0.02-0.04 s per completion from D-45's measured step times) = **2-4 GPU-h**. Total **~6-12 GPU-h** and 2-3 days of data engineering (Seed alignment, mined-pair filtering, calibration entries from the human sets, LID label sets, language rows).
* **Calibration per language** (the D-43 recipe, one day each): content mode (consensus or blend), `lid` block (labels incl. all Fula varieties, min chars), length / omission / diacritic / lexical blocks from the human sets, weight by the content-rho rule (>= 0.25 -> 0.5), a QE entry only where AUC >= 0.70 (fuv, gaa, knc).

## 6. The owner's idea: stop at the end of b1024, add languages, continue through b2048 and mixed

(Technical content: the code paths read in `rl/train_rl.py`, `rl/data.py`, the staged D-45 yaml and the b2048 report.)

**What the trainer does:** languages are the rows of `languages.py` with `rl_eligible` (`SUPPORTED_RL`); `load_rl_prompts` drops any prompt row whose iso is not in it; `PromptSchedule` draws a language with probability proportional to `n_l^alpha x language_weights` (alpha 0 -> `w_l / sum w`, **sum w = 104**: 59 x 1 + 18 x 2.5), stage by stage for ALL languages at once (b256 391 steps, b512 446, b1024 149, then b2048 298 and mixed 149 in the staged D-45 config); the KL reference is `load_model(cfg.model_name)` = the starting model of the PHASE, also after `resume`; the calibration file gives `content`, `lid`, `reliability` and the shaping blocks per language, and **a language without an entry gets default anchors and weight 1.0**.

**What adding K languages at the b1024 -> b2048 restart needs (nothing applied):**
1. `languages.py`: K appended rows (nde fat knc gaa men luy tzm ful already exist without RL flags; `fuv` is the table's `ful`), `rl_consensus=True` or LISTED; the display name is part of the prompt and can never change.
2. A prompt directory `rl_prompts_v4` = v3 hard links + `zz_newlang/<iso>.parquet` (English windows re-labelled from the existing pools, rows in EVERY bin the schedule will still visit, otherwise the language silently drops out of that stage), same recipe as D-45 so old row indices stay.
3. **A way to write the tag:** the policy has never produced `fuv`; with `prefix_policy: require` every sample is `bad_prefix`. Either an SFT top-up teaches the tag, or the trainer forces the tag into the prompt for these languages (the probe's `prefill` condition) and the reward skips the tag check; neither exists in the code today.
4. Calibration v7 (entries per new language) and `language_weights`, `calibration_path`, `prompts_path`, `steps` in a staged yaml, copied over the live yaml at a checkpoint restart (`resume: true`), exactly as D-45.

**Cost to the main run (arithmetic on 12,516 prompts left in b2048 + mixed):** K = 4 new languages at weight 1 take 3.7 % of all draws (116 prompts each); K = 8: 7.1 % (112 each); K = 8 at weight 2.5: 16.1 % (252 each); K = 16: 13.3 % (104 each). Every old language loses the same share. Each new language would see 100-250 prompts = 1.7-4 k samples (the old languages saw hundreds of thousands), **only on the longest windows** (b2048: 1,025-2,048 AfriCOMET tokens, ~44 sentences, up to 7,168 new tokens; mixed: one fifth each), because the curriculum is by bin for all languages; a language that cannot be written produces thousands of tokens of junk per sample there and the step time follows the longest completion (10 ms per token; 72 s at the cap). If the new languages were writable their tags would still be new tokens against a reference that never wrote them (KL).

**Verdict.** Technically possible, but it gives the languages the worst possible training conditions (long windows only, ~100 prompts, no tag, no SFT), costs 4-16 % of the remaining draws of the 77 trained languages (about 1-2 h of the 17-h b2048 stage per 8 weight-1 languages), and by the probe nothing would be learned: all samples fail the GlotLID guard, the groups are flat. The right order is **SFT top-up (or the inter-African SFT) -> new reference model -> short b64/b256 RL phase for the new languages with replay of the old ones -> only then the common curriculum**. If the owner wants the languages in the b2048 / mixed stages anyway, the only safe variant is: weight 0 in the calibration for each new language (so they cost draws but never produce gradient) until the SFT is done — which is strictly worse than leaving them out.

## 7. A measured CPU micro top-up (Fula)

`scripts/newlang_cpu_topup.py`, results `data/topup_fuv.json` (run 1, evaluations at 0 / 25 / 50 / 100 %) and `data/topup_fuv_run2.json` + `data/topup_probe_summary.json` (run 2, identical training, then the full probe). Setup: the RL step-925 policy, all weights trained (fp32, AdamW lr 2e-5, batch 16, 213 steps = **1 epoch over 3,100 human English-Fula pairs of NLLB-Seed `fuv_Latn` (CC-BY-SA) + 305 replay pairs (the model's own greedy outputs for yor hau swh wol bem ach kbp nus)**), table row `ful` / name "Fulah" so that no row is added; 16 threads, **37 minutes of CPU, 1.5 M tokens**. Evaluation on the 60 FLORES+ dev fuv sentences (not in the training pairs; the Seed English is Wikipedia) with free generation (the model must write the tag itself) and the usual metrics.

| after | pairs seen | writes tag `ful` | GlotLID Fula | finished | greedy chrF++ (floor 8.8) | yor | hau | wol | kbp (chrF++ on 30 sentences) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (RL policy) | 0 | 0 % | 7 % (chance hits) | 97 % | 12.9 | 23.4 | 47.1 | 28.5 | 19.6 |
| 25 % | 848 | 100 % | 72 % | 48 % | 5.5 | 23.7 | 43.8 | 26.1 | 15.2 |
| 50 % | 1,696 | 100 % | 97 % | 65 % | 10.2 | 21.2 | 41.3 | 24.8 | 12.8 |
| 100 % | 3,404 | **100 %** | **98 %** (fuv 54 / 60) | 85 % | **13.2** | 20.9 | 41.1 | 23.6 | 11.6 |

* **Identity is learned from a few hundred pairs; translation is not learned from 3,100.** The tag and the language identity are acquired within ~850 pairs (tag 100 %, GlotLID Fula 72 %), at 1,700 pairs the output is Fula (97 %), but the chrF++ stays at the level of the unrelated-text floor + 4 (13.2 vs 12.9 before, 8.8 floor) and 15 % of the greedy outputs still end in a loop ("Ɗum ɗum ɗum ..."). SmolSent `ff`: 10.1 (before 10.3). One epoch of ~3 k pairs is therefore a gate-opener, not a translator: the 5-10 k-pair estimate of section 5.2 is a lower bound for identity, not for quality, and needs several epochs / more pairs (Fula is agglutinative with noun classes; the Seed sentences are Wikipedia biographies).
* **Forgetting is large with this recipe**: -2.5 (yor), -6.0 (hau), -4.9 (wol), -8.0 (kbp) chrF++ on 30 sentences after one epoch at lr 2e-5 with ~9 % replay. A real top-up needs the replay share, learning rate and mixing of section 5.2 (>= 50 % replay of the original windows, lr <= 1e-5) and the macro dev check.
* **This is the dangerous middle state, and the confidence rules handle it.** Probe of the post-top-up model (60 sentences x [greedy + 4 samples], same metrics): GlotLID right at T = 1 **100 %**, valid rate **96.7 %**, 98 % of the groups pass the gate (>= 3 valid, LID, distinct), consensus level 16.2. A reward with the GlotLID guard and the D-38 consensus would now PAY for these samples. But: **within-prompt rho(consensus, chrF++) = 0.30, with the chrF++ against an UNRELATED reference 0.47, content-only -0.04 (se 0.07, 59 groups)** (trained languages: +0.26), and the mean token log-prob of the T = 1 samples is **-3.99** (the trained range is -0.4 .. -2.7; greedy -1.04). The log-prob arm of the trust rule gives **trust 0** (absolute and run-relative), so the advantage weight stays 0 although GlotLID and the valid rate are perfect. Without the log-prob arm the rule would have opened the gate for a language whose consensus carries no content signal. (One language, one checkpoint, 59 groups: an existence proof of the failure mode and of the catch, not a calibration.)
* Cost: 37 CPU-minutes for 213 steps (10 s per step of 16 sequences of ~100 tokens, 16 threads); on a GPU one epoch of this size should take minutes (not measured); the data preparation (Seed alignment, filtering, calibration) is the work, not the training.

## 8. Uncertainties and what was not measured

* 60 sentences per language: chrF++ SE 0.3-1.8; the QE validity AUCs have SE ~0.04 at 150 pairs. Rankings between languages that differ by < 2 chrF++ excess are noise.
* G = 5 (greedy + 4 samples) instead of the RL's 16 (the within-prompt rho and the number of valid members are therefore not the RL's; the D-43 numbers at G = 8 agree with ours at G = 5 within 0.07).
* One checkpoint of the RL run (step 925, b512/b1024 stage) and one early SFT checkpoint; the features are checkpoint-specific (log-prob). Long-window behaviour (b1024 / b2048) of the junk was not probed (finished rate 100 % at sentence length).
* GlotLID recall was measured on the same human sets whose language it classifies; the held-out status of FLORES dev against the SFT corpus was not re-checked (D-42 found 91 % of SmolSent English in the training windows for the 45 trained languages; for the NEW languages the English sentences may be in the training windows via other languages, which would only make the zero result more generous).
* Speaker numbers, JW row counts (estimated from CSV bytes), the SFT throughput on the RTX PRO 6000 and the GPU-hour totals are estimates, labelled as such.
* The licence column is what the Hub card declares, not a legal clearance.
