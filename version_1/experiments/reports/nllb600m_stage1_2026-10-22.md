## 0. Answer for the owner

**Does NLLB work well on our languages?** Where it covers a language, yes, and the 3.3B model clearly more than the 600M one. On FLORES+ devtest (English -> X, sentence level, rows whose reference pair our SFT never saw) over the 44 languages both cover, chrF++ is **ours (final_1433, greedy) 34.3, NLLB-600M beam 4 35.9 (+1.7 [+1.6, +1.8]), NLLB-3.3B beam 4 38.1 (+3.8 [+3.8, +3.9]; spBLEU +4.4)**. The 3.3B advantage holds on the other human benchmarks (NTREX +4.2, MAFAND +3.5, SmolSent +5.5, SALT +3.5) and has the same sign as on FLORES+ in 64 of 73 language x benchmark pairs. The 600M model is about at our level (greedy 600M: +0.4); it is clearly better only for Ge'ez/Cushitic/Berber (+4.7) and Nilo-Saharan (+2.8) and is *worse* than us in Atlantic-Congo non-Bantu (-1.5; Wolof -6.3, Fon -3.7, Twi -2.4, Yoruba -2.1). The 3.3B model is better than ours by >= +5 chrF++ in 18 languages (Kabuverdianu +21.1, Amharic +18.9, Tigrinya +11.0, Kanuri +9.7 (we have no Kanuri data), Nuer +7.9, Kirundi +7.7, Ewe +7.2, Kabiye +7.0, Malagasy +7.0, Kinyarwanda +6.2, Bambara +6.0, ...), by +2..+5 in 8, within +-2 in 17 and worse by more than 2 in two (Umbundu -2.5, Dyula -2.8; Kamba, Wolof, Kimbundu, Fon, Yoruba are -1 to -2): **do not use it as teacher or signal for those.** The gain is largest where we are weakest (Ge'ez, Nilo-Saharan, Afroasiatic +7.2 on average) and smallest in Atlantic-Congo non-Bantu (+1.4).

**Where it is useless: coverage.** NLLB-200 covers **44 of our 77 RL languages and 2 of 8 SFT-only ones** (46 of 85 table rows exactly; Congo Swahili, Fante and Fulah only through a related variety). It does not cover **22 of the 23 consensus-only languages** (only Kabuverdianu is covered), 8 of the 12 listed/blend languages (Dyula, Sango and Kongo are covered), Pidgin, Efik, Ibibio, Tiv, Venda, Edo, Dagbani, Okpe, Tachelhit, Ghomala, Kaonde, Lunda, Isoko, Lozi, Wolaytta, Acholi, Tswa, Makhuwa, Kuanyama, Sena, Nyankore, Herero, Yao, Sidamo, Ndau, Krio, Esan, Duala, Basaa, Cameroon Pidgin, Ndebele, Ga, Mende, Luyia. Of the 8 weakest languages (`ish bin bbj tsc kon nus dag oke`) only `kon` and `nus` are covered. NLLB therefore cannot replace curation for the languages with the weakest reward signal. Kongo's +31 on SmolSent is mostly a *variety* effect (NLLB writes the Kituba-like Kikongo of the references, our model another variety).

## 2. Pipeline and check

Models: NLLB-200-distilled-600M converted by us to CTranslate2 float16; **NLLB-200-3.3B = the Hub build `OpenNMT/nllb-200-3.3B-ct2-int8`** (CTranslate2 authors, 3.4 GB; the fp32 checkpoint was never downloaded), `int8_float16`. Beam 4, `max_decoding_length` 256, sentence level, source `eng_Latn`. **Check against the published NLLB-200-3.3B FLORES-200 devtest chrF++** (`published_baselines.csv`, all 1,012 rows, NFC scoring): over 43 languages mean difference -0.17, **mean absolute 0.40 chrF++**, maximum +3.0 (Yoruba: the repo scores NFC text and 28 % of the Yoruba references are decomposed). Scoring = `gemma_mt.eval.metrics` (chrF++ sacrebleu word_order 2, spBLEU flores200 tokenizer, NFC); ours = a fresh run of `final_1433` (greedy, vLLM, 768 tokens) on the same rows, so that sentence-level paired bootstrap (1,000 resamples, segment statistics summed) is possible; macro 34.25 vs 34.1 in the existing per-language CSV (45-language set). Subsets as in our table (`all`, `no_pair`, `clean`).

## 5. Caveats
* English -> X only, sentence level; NLLB fails on long inputs, so documents must be split.
* NLLB was tuned on FLORES-200 dev and trained on a large Wikimedia-heavy mined corpus: FLORES+ is the most favourable benchmark for it; NTREX (news), MAFAND (news), SmolSent and SALT (agriculture) show the same sign with +3.5..+5.5.
* Beam 4 (NLLB) vs greedy (ours) is the comparison a teacher or pseudo-reference would use; the 600M greedy row isolates the decoding effect (-1.3). 3.3B greedy was not run (GPU contention).
* One checkpoint of ours (`final_1433`), one reference per sentence; chrF++ differences below ~2 are within what a reward signal can resolve. No human judgement was used.
