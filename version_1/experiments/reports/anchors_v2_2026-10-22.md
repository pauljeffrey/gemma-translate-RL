## 2. (1) Number and name preservation for more languages

Human drop rate `d` per language and channel: share of the judged human sentences (numbers: >= 1 number in the source; names: >= 3 capitalised non-initial names) whose reference lost every entity of the channel, measured on FLORES+ dev, SmolSent, NTREX, IbomMT, SALT, flores_moz, glot_story (90,349 sentence units, 64 languages; devtest untouched). Examples: numbers fon 3 %, umb 0 %, kab 0 %, yor 34 %, efi 31 %, dyu 56 %, kik 60 % (off), ibb 68 % (off); names bem 2 %, nya 3 %, hau 9 %, fon 15 %, mos 18 %, kab 41 % (separation too small, off), amh / tir / shi 83-97 % (script transliteration, off).

Rule: `penalty = anchors_entity_weight x sum_c n_c w_c (1 - recall_c) / sum_c n_c`, `w_num = 1 - d`, `w_name = 0.5 (1 - d)`; languages with < 60 judged chunks shrink `d` towards the pooled median (m = 20); languages without references use the pooled 90th-percentile `d` and half the weight (numbers w 0.41, names 0.15); the 12 weak languages without any human reference are calibrated provisionally on the held-out rows of R3's pivot corpus (300-600 sentences, mostly religious text; flagged `basis: pivot_held` in the blocks).

Result (full table: `table_langs.md`; `entity2_report.md`): numbers enabled in 67 of 74 languages (off: ibb kqn lun loz kua her bin), names in 54 of 76. Weak languages with a channel: v1 3 (+swc), v2 **39 of 45** (27 on human references, 12 provisional; not covered: bbj dag her kqn kua tsc, too few numbered / named sentences). Policy samples of the final model vs the human references of the same sources (x0.5 weight): mean penalty 0.056 vs 0.068 over 45 languages; amh / tir policy 0.09 / 0.15 vs human 0.00 / 0.04 (the model drops digits where humans keep them).

Honest caveats. (a) The requested "FP <= 1-2 % per language" is a threshold concept; with a graded penalty the share of humans penalised is about 50 % (any missing entity) by construction. What is bounded is the size: mean 0.07, <= 0.5 x w, and the weight falls with `d`. (b) In GRPO a penalty on missing entities is the same, up to a per-group constant, as a bonus for retaining them, which pushes towards copying; the copy cap (section 3) and the lexical penalty (D-25) are the counterweights, and the weight (0.5 x w) is small. (c) Names use a capitalisation heuristic: spelling variants (one edit allowed for names >= 5 letters) are tolerated, genuine re-spellings are counted as dropped.

## 3. (2) Copy detection by substring

Definition (`textstats.copy_fractions`): source tokens = `\w+` runs of the NFKD, mark-stripped text (Ge'ez safe, U+1361 / 1362 are punctuation); protected (never counted): tokens with digits, URLs / e-mails, capitalised words that do not start a sentence (also after "Dr." / "J."), a sentence-initial capitalised word followed by another capitalised one, all-caps acronyms, lowercase connectors between two protected tokens ("University of Oxford"), and the language's learned loanword whitelist (source words >= 4 letters that the human reference keeps in >= 50 % of >= 4 occurrences; median 4 words, max 30 per language, learned out-of-fold). `frac_w` = share of the unprotected source words covered by a window of 3 consecutive source words that occurs as 3 consecutive output words (>= 2 unprotected words in the window); `frac_c` = same for 20-character windows over the normalised token string; the copied fraction is the larger one that exceeds its threshold. Threshold per language `t` = max(0.08, 99.5 % quantile of the human fraction); a language is enabled when the 2-fold CV FP <= 1.2 % and `t_w <= 0.45`. Pooled prior (no references): the 90th percentile of the thresholds (0.17).

Penalty: the chunk reward becomes `max(failure penalty, min(reward, -2 x fraction))` (`anchors_copy_scale`, mode `cap`; `subtract` = reward - 2 x fraction is the alternative). Fraction 1 gives -2; 0.5 gives -1.0 even when the rest of the chunk is a perfect translation; below `t` nothing happens (a step from 0 to -2t at the threshold: median t 0.08, max 0.27). The appended whole source is fraction 1.0 (the metric is "share of the SOURCE copied"), so translation + appended source costs the same as a pure copy.

Precedence (no double penalty): (1) `guards.is_copy_of_source` (Jaccard >= 0.9 on the whole sample) = failure class `copy_source`, reward -2, no chunk plan, never reaches the new detector; (2) `plan_chunks`' per-chunk Jaccard copy = failed chunk with its weight, not a scored segment, so the cap never sees it; (3) the new detector acts on scored chunks only and floors at the failure penalty. The two rules measure different things (word-set overlap of the whole sample vs verbatim runs per chunk) and cover disjoint cases above / below Jaccard 0.9, so unifying them was not necessary.

Measured (fold-B human sentences, calibration fitted on fold A; `eval_summary_sent.md`, `eval_summary_docs10.md`): FP 0.5 % mean (max 1.3 %, no language above 2 %) at sentence level, 0.0 % (max 1.5 %) on 10-sentence documents; on FLORES+ dev alone 2 % (humans keep more English there; the threshold is a pooled quantile over all sets). Corruption classes of A5 (FLORES+ dev): copy_source 100 % (also the hard gate), copy_noisy 99 %, append_source 100 %, mix_half_src 100 %, swap25_src 2 % (isolated swapped words are below k = 3: by design not a substring; the English-function-word share of `func` finds 57 % of them), append_unrelated / mismatch / drop_half / replace_words / shuffle 0-1 %. Synthetic copied tails of the human references (calibration, in-sample): 25 % tail 78 % detected on average (median 84 %, min 27 %), 50 % tail 96 % (min 47 %). Not usable: pcm and wes (English-based: the human reference shares 80 % with the source; threshold 0.8), xho (FP), ibb. English-heavy output (`en_copy`) is found by copy (AUC 1.00) and by the English-function-word share of `func` (0.99).

## 4. (3) Stop-word profile

Dictionary: for every language the 80 words with the highest geometric mean of the document frequency in (a) the target-language TRAINING text (R4's packed SFT / replay mixture decoded with the model tokenizer + R3's pivot corpus, 0.04-1.6 M tokens per language; science and Bible domains) and (b) the human references (so domain words like "researchers" or "Jehovah" drop out). English function words (a 150-word list) that the language itself uses (>= 0.01 % of its training tokens, or in its dictionary) are excluded from the English-leakage count. Features per output (>= 8 words, source >= 5 words): target function-word share, English-only function-word share, target function words per English function word of the source (`ratio`), and the share above the human high quantile (bag of function words). Thresholds per length bucket (<= 7, 8-15, 16-30, 31-60, > 60 words) at the human 1 % quantile moved outwards by the smallest margin (0 .. 4 tail distances) that gives a 2-fold CV FP of the union <= 2 % (Ge'ez: 0.5 %). Deficit 0 at the threshold, 1 at a quarter of it (English share: at 0.30).

Measured (fold B; `table_langs.md` has FP and AUC per language):

| | sentences (400 per language) | documents of 10 sentences (~200 words) |
|---|---|---|
| FP on human references, mean / max over 42 judged languages | 1.2 % / 3.8 % (7 languages above 2 %: tsn 3.8, kea 3.3, seh 2.8 ...) | 0.6 % / 10.5 % (4 above 2 %) |
| AUC vs English copy | 0.99 | 1.00 |
| AUC vs stuffed content words (`stuff`) | 0.97 | 1.00 |
| AUC vs other-language text | 0.88 | 0.96 |
| AUC vs R3 padded attack / function-word bag | 0.84 / 0.97 | 0.89 / 1.00 |
| AUC vs adaptive bag with the human function-word share (`stuff_fw`) | 0.48 | 0.40 |

**Usable languages** (enabled in the calibration: block `func.enabled`, FP_cv <= 2 % and detection of stuffed text >= 50 %): 55 of 77, among the weak ones bam dyu efi fon iso kab kam kbp kmb kon mos nus sag swc tiv ven (blend) and ach bas bbj bin dua ibb ish kea kri oke seh tsc urh vmw wes yao (consensus; bas bbj bin dua ish iso oke swc urh wes yao provisional). **Not usable:** amh tir (Ge'ez, strict target; the dictionary words are mostly attached morphemes), zul xho sna ssw nbl ndc run lug orm (agglutinative: almost no free function words, human share too variable), nyn shi umb, kqn kua loz lun her dag sid wal (< 600 sentences or too little training text). The source-English-vs-target ratio check is part of the same deficit (its marginal value was not isolated; on its own it behaves like the share check). Weight: `anchors_func_weight` (default 0.3, "small"); the profile alone cannot stop a bag that mimics the human share (`stuff_fw`).

### 5.3 Consensus attractor simulation (does a stuffed output win a GRPO group?)

`attractor_study.py`: for 12 weak languages with a bigram table or lexicon, held-out FLORES+ dev prompts, groups of 8 made of real rl2c samples (A5, T = 1) in which k = 2 / 4 / 6 members are replaced by the same attack text and one by the human reference; reward = consensus (mean chrF++ vs the other members -> [-2, 2.25]) minus penalties, floored at -2, hard gates = -2; "adv > 0" = share of prompts in which the attackers' group-normalised advantage is positive (they would be reinforced). Per-case tables: `attractor_sent_summary.md` (sentences), `attractor_docs_summary.md` (documents of 10 sentences, 9 per language).

Findings. (i) Without any anchor, k = 4 identical attackers beat the honest members (raw reward +0.2 .. +0.5 vs -0.5 .. -1.2; adv > 0 in 95-100 % of the prompts): **the consensus signal has a real attractor on any deterministic output that is not rejected by a guard**; at k = 2 the attack still loses or ties (adv > 0 in 0-73 %). (ii) Existing gates already kill document-level `stuff30`, `pad` and `gloss` (length-ratio gate: attack reward -2, adv > 0 0-3 %). (iii) Sentence level: with func 1 / pad 1 the attacks lose at k = 2 (`stuff30` adv > 0 7 %, `pad_fit` 10 %) but still win at k = 4 (84 % / 94 %) and k = 6; with func 2 / pad 2 `stuff30@4` (17 %) and `pad_fit@4` (28 %) flip, k = 6 does not (98-99 %), because the profile is off in some of the 12 languages. (iv) Remaining at document level, k = 4, with every penalty on: `pad_fit` adv > 0 95 %, `gloss_pad` 100 %, `stuff_fw` 73-83 % (func 2 / pad 2: 73 %); the attackers' reward stays +0.1 .. +0.4 against -0.5 for honest members. These are the length-matched natural-filler and the profile-matching bag attacks. (v) The hard `anchors_stuff_gate` (0.9) removes saturated bags from the consensus pool and gives them -2: sentence-level `stuff30@4` adv > 0 100 % -> 17 %, `pad_fit@6` 100 % -> 31 %; it does nothing for (iv).

What closes the rest (not built, honest): a content check that does not exist in the consensus-only languages. The attackers in (iv) are prompt-specific (filler taken from a different prompt's reference); a policy that emits ONE stock text for all prompts is caught by D-56's cross-prompt boilerplate buffer (100 % in the A/B of R2). The real defences are the QE-side rank order (5.2), the boilerplate buffer, the `extra_output` / length rules, the lexicon term in 10 languages, and watching `reward/anchors/*`, `fail/stuffed_output` and the per-language `bench_aux` chrF++ (a collapse onto a fixed attractor shows as flat groups and a falling `weak_dev` score).

### 5.4 Recommendation for weights

`anchors_func_weight` 0.3 (owner: small) is enough for the clear cases at document level (combined 1.4-2.0 with the other gates); `anchors_pad_weight` 1.0; `anchors_stuff_gate` 0.0 for the first launch and **0.9 as the first thing to switch on if `anchors/func/penalised_rate` or `anchors/pad/penalised_rate` of a consensus-only language climbs** (measured FP of the gate on held-out human references: stop-word deficit >= 0.9 in 0.07 % of 24,121 sentences and 0 of 4,465 documents, padding deficit >= 0.9 in 0.02 % / 0). Weights above 1 for func / pad were tested (func 2, pad 2): they flip more sentence-level cases but cost up to 2 x deficit on the 0.6-1.2 % of humans flagged; not recommended before a first look at the logs.

## 6. (5) Zero-tolerance filter: what happens today, and a hard mask

Today (verified in `reward.py` and `train_rl.py` of the R7 tree): a sample that fails a hard check (`no_end_of_turn`, `post_eos_rubbish`, `dangling_marker`, `special_tokens`, `bad_prefix`, `empty`, `copy_source`, `degenerate`, `length_ratio`, `wrong_language`, and now `stuffed_output`) gets `class_penalty.get(status, penalty)` = -2.0 (the floor of the `repo` range; EOS classes may be more negative by config), no QE call, no consensus membership, and no anchors (the sample has no chunk plan). Soft penalties (anchors, length, lexical, diacritics, science anchor) are `max(penalty, r - w x deficit)`: floored at the same -2. A chunk of a longer sample that fails (omitted / copied / appended) enters `aggregate` with weight x penalty, i.e. a failed chunk, not a failed sample. The trainer then computes `grpo.group_advantages` (mean / std over ALL members of the group, `std_eps` 1e-4, flat groups -> 0) and multiplies by the per-language reliability weight only (`adv * out.weights`); `status` is not looked at. So a -2 sample in a group of ~0.5 rewards has advantage about -2 .. -3 (3.1 at a 3 % failure rate in the toy model below): the strongest negative signal in the group. **It is not removed, and masking would remove the learning signal that teaches the policy to stop failing** (the log-probability of a failed sequence is pushed down; the survivors only get a mild positive push).

Option implemented (OFF, `hard_fail_mask`): `zero` = failed advantage set to 0, group mean / std unchanged; `exclude` = mean / std over the survivors only, failed members 0 (groups with < 2 survivors are flat). Toy model (`hardfail_study.py`, two-outcome softmax policy, G = 16, failure reward -2, success ~ N(0.5, 0.3)), expected gradient on the FAILURE logit (negative lowers P(fail)): at 3 % failure standard -0.093, zero -0.003, exclude 0.000; at 10 % -0.242 / -0.024 / 0.000; at 25 % -0.400 / -0.100 / 0.000; at 50 % -0.477 / -0.239 / 0.000. The mean |advantage| of a failed sample is 3.1 / 2.4 / 1.6 / 0.95 under the standard rule. Masking therefore leaves the failure rate to drift (truncation and wrong-language outputs are only ever punished through this signal). Recommendation: keep `off`. If the owner wants failures out of the update anyway, `zero` is the milder variant (the policy still learns from the contrast with the survivors). The KL term and the token normaliser still include the masked samples. Logged: `hard_fail_frac`. Tests: `tests/test_hard_fail_mask.py`. YAML note: an unquoted `off` is read as the boolean False; the validator maps it to "off".

## 12. Risks and weak evidence

1. Entity soft mode: about half of the humans' numbered / named sentences get a small penalty; the effect on GRPO is relative (retention is rewarded), weight 0.5 x w; watch `anchors/entity2/penalised_rate`. The strict mode (v1) is one key away but global.
2. Copy: the step from 0 to -2t at the threshold is discontinuous (median t 0.08): a chunk that keeps 9 % of the source as verbatim lowercase English phrases (untranslated technical terms) loses ~1.4 .. 2.0 of reward; humans do that in 0.5 % of sentences. The policy's own rate was not measured on the final model's real outputs here (no new generation); measure `anchors/copy/penalised_rate` in the first 50 steps. The 12 provisional pivot languages have thresholds from religious text.
3. Stop-word profile: unusable in 22 languages; 7 languages exceed 2 % held-out FP at sentence level (CV estimate was <= 2 %: the cross-validated number has ~1 pt of noise, `table_langs.md`).
4. Padding: the bigram-order feature works only at document length and only where the training text matches the test domain (about 20 languages); `pad_fit`, `gloss_pad` and a profile-matching bag remain unpunished by any text statistic, and win a consensus group in the simulation from k = 4 of 8 on. Evidence is simulated (identical prompt-specific attackers, the worst case for consensus); no real policy produces such outputs today.
5. Dictionaries and bigram tables come from the SFT / replay text (Bible, JW, science, news-like), thresholds from FLORES+ / SmolSent / NTREX: a domain gap that the geometric-mean selection and the shuffled-baseline gain reduce but do not remove.
6. Languages without human references (12 weak ones) are calibrated on 300-600 held-out pivot sentences (religious): provisional; switch them off with `anchors_v2_off_languages` if their `penalised_rate` is odd.
7. The 39 / 45 entity and 32 / 45 stop-word coverage of the weak languages include 12 / 11 provisional ones; on a human-reference basis it is 27 / 21.
8. `anchors_v2_modes` includes `qe`: copy / stop-word / padding also act on the 32 QE languages (FP 0.5 % / 1.2 % / 1.2 %); remove `qe` from the list if the owner wants the v2 guards on the weak languages only.
