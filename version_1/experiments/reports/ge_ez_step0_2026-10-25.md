## 1. Tokenizer fertility (tested)

Tokenizers: our model's tokenizer (`<HF_ORG_LEGACY>/gemma-3-270m-translate-rl2c`, `latest/tokenizer.json`) vs `facebook/nllb-200-distilled-600M` (tokenizer only). Text: FLORES+ **devtest**, 1,012 human sentences per language. Counts exclude bos/eos/language tag. Word = whitespace token. Byte fallback = tokens `<0xNN>`. Ratio = Gemma tokens / NLLB tokens, 95% sentence-bootstrap interval.

**Same vocabulary in 270M, 1B and 4B (tested):** the vocabulary (262,144 entries) and the 514,906 merges are byte-for-byte equal in our rl2c tokenizer, `google/gemma-3-270m-it`, `-1b-it` and `-4b-it` (vocab hash 6f8b69b0f1fda277, merges hash e801c86280431857). The 1B and 4B `tokenizer.json` files are identical; the 270M differs by 2 bytes (one flag on `<mask>`). So a vocabulary change planned for the 270M carries over to 1B/4B without a new tokenizer.

Reading: our tokenizer needs 1.29 to 1.53 times NLLB's tokens per sentence on the eight African languages (mean 1.39; the five Latin controls average 1.36, matching the owner's 1.35). Ge'ez is not far above that: amh (1.31) is below the average, tir (1.53) is the highest, but fon (1.47), ibo (1.45) and yor (1.41) are close behind. In tokens per character Ge'ez is expensive (0.60 to 0.74 vs 0.32 to 0.37 for hau, swh, kin) but NLLB's own tokenizer also spends 0.46 to 0.49 per Ethiopic character, which is why the ratio to NLLB is ordinary. So tokenization costs sequence length (about 30 to 50% more output tokens than NLLB for these languages) but cannot by itself explain a 19 chrF++ gap on amh (19.0 vs 38.0); for amh it is below the average penalty. The existing report (`weak_language_data_2026-10-22.md`, section 4) measured the same per-character/per-word figures (amh 3.03, tir 3.46): reproduced exactly.

### 2c. Simulation of N new tokens (tested; no model, no quality claim)
Method (continued BPE on the existing tokenizer, then a real modified `tokenizer.json` loaded in HF `tokenizers` and applied to the references): (0) Ethiopic letters without a token get a single-character token (65 are missing; those seen at least 3 times in the training text, 71 to 86 letters); (1) the training text is tokenised with the existing tokenizer and, inside words, the most frequent adjacent token pair is merged repeatedly (min count 5); each merge becomes a new merge rule appended at the END of the merge list plus a new vocabulary entry in a freed id (unused tail first; beyond 6,143 the smallest scripts are freed with their merge rules, closure computed). Merges are restricted so that old text cannot trigger them: for Ge'ez a merge must contain an Ethiopic letter and no Latin letter; for Fon (`fon_strict`) a letter beyond Latin-1 not used by Yoruba (ɖ ɛ ɔ ŋ ɣ ǎ ...), (`fon` = any non-ASCII letter, shown for contrast). Training text (never FLORES devtest; 0 of 1,012 devtest sentences found verbatim in any training text): amh 25.2 M characters (Wikipedia am + FineTranslations amh original text), tir 25.2 M (Wikipedia ti + FineTranslations tir), Ge'ez joint = equal amounts of both, fon 6.2 M (Wikipedia fon + FineWeb-2 fon + FLORES+ fon dev). Caveat: FineTranslations is web text (jw.org style is a large part for amh/tir), devtest is news/wiki: a domain gap, but the measure is on held-out text.

Old text re-tokenised (tested, devtest, N=8,000; share of sentences whose tokenisation changed): Ge'ez variants change **0.0%** of English, French, hau, yor, swh, kin, ibo, ewe, bam, lin, wol, zul, kik, som and fon sentences (by construction and measured). The Fon variants change Fon 100%, and also ewe 98%, bam 92% (`fon_strict`; ɛ ɔ ɖ are shared; token counts fall by 12% for ewe), hau 8%; `fon_strict` leaves French, Yoruba, Wolof, Igbo and Lingala at 0.0% (the looser `fon` vocabulary changes Yoruba 75%, French 26%, Wolof 23%). Decoding round trip equals the base decoding for all checked sets (min 1.0). So Ge'ez tokens are risk-free for the rest of the 77 languages; Fon tokens touch Ewe and Bambara, which would have to be in the retraining mix.

### 2d. Procedure (plan; the tokenizer parts are tested as marked)
1. Choose slots: ids 256001 to 262143 (the `<unused99>`..`<unused6241>` tail). Nothing in the repo's chat template or training code uses them (`<unusedNN>` appears only in a text-sanitising regex, a fake tokenizer and tests). Ids 6 to 104 and all ids < 107 stay untouched, so special tokens do not move.
2. `tokenizer.json` (tested): for each new token delete the old string from `model.vocab` and put the new string at that id; delete the same id from `added_tokens` (the `<unusedN>` entries are listed there and would otherwise intercept); append the new merge rules `[a, b]` at the end of `model.merges` in learning order (lowest priority, so old tokenisation is unchanged except for text containing the new pairs); single-character tokens need no merge. Never reuse an id that occurs in an existing merge unless that merge and every token that depends on it are removed together (a first attempt that freed Devanagari ids made the file unloadable, which is why the script computes the closure).
3. `tokenizer.model` (SentencePiece, the file llama.cpp's converter reads; tested at tokenizer level): for the same ids set `piece` = new string, `type` = NORMAL (1), `score` = (lowest existing score -255,504) minus rank, so new merges have lower priority than all old ones. With N=3,000 Ge'ez tokens SentencePiece and `tokenizers` give identical ids on 4 devtest sets (amh, tir, eng, hau: 100% of 1,012 sentences each). Not tested: llama.cpp conversion and GGUF load, vLLM load, the Transformers fast-tokenizer load of the edited file.
4. Embeddings (plan, untested): the matrix is tied (input = output head) and is multiplied by sqrt(hidden) at input. Initialise each new row from the old tokenisation of the new string: mean of the rows of its two parts (for a merge) or of its 2 to 3 byte tokens (for a single character); alternatives are the sum scaled to the mean old norm, or a small random row. Do not trust the current `<unused>` rows.
5. Retraining (plan): at least the new rows plus a short SFT on amh/tir/fon English->X pairs, re-tokenised with the new tokenizer (all old Ethiopic/Fon SFT data must be re-tokenised; old checkpoints see different ids for the same text); a first stage with only the embedding/head rows trainable then a normal SFT is the cheap version; whether LoRA-free full SFT is needed is unknown. Size of the retraining is not estimated here.
6. Risks: (a) old checkpoints and the existing RL archive are not compatible with the new ids for Ge'ez/Fon text; (b) GGUF/llama.cpp, vLLM and CTranslate2 conversions must be re-run and checked (llama.cpp reads SentencePiece scores, so the lowest-score rule above matters); (c) vocabulary pruning: the existing keep list (159,629 ids) excludes the tail, so a pruned model needs the new ids added to the keep list and renumbered (the lite gain of 1.235x does not change); (d) the retrained rows start worse than the old pieces, so the first checkpoints can be worse on amh/tir/fon; (e) JW-heavy training text may give tokens that fit that domain; (f) the shorter outputs speed decoding by about the token saving but quality is unknown.

## 4. Training-cost scaling (estimates derived from the 270M record)

Base record (paper Appendix F, USD 1.19/h): SFT 27,638 steps x 6.4 s = 49.1 h; RL 2c 40.5 h; final run 28.4 h; science SFT 17 to 35 min; **RL phases 1, 2, 2b have no recorded hours**. Formula: P_eff = transformer + head parameters (270M 268 M, 1B 1,000 M = 3.73x, 4B 3,884 M = 14.5x). Step-time multiplier m = low / central / high 1.8 / 2.7 / 3.7 (1B) and 5 / 9 / 14.5 (4B): high = pure FLOP ratio; lower because the 270M run uses the GPU poorly (about 33 TFLOP/s effective by 6ND; a 640-wide model does not fill the card), the same reasoning as in `gemma_rl_cost_estimate_2026-10-17.md`. RL step = fixed part (reward models, LID, consensus, sync: 20% / 12% / 5% of the step, unchanged) + the rest x m; sampling is 49 to 57% of the logged step (`t_sample`). Steps and tokens as in the 270M run. 4B memory: fp32 policy + grads + AdamW = 62 GB plus an fp32 reference (15.5 GB) plus vLLM does not fit 96 GB; factor 1.0 / 1.15 / 1.3 for bf16 reference, 8-bit optimiser or a second card. Unrecorded RL phases: 12 h (if cost follows generated tokens) to 39 h (if it follows completions), central 18 h. GPU-hours (USD in brackets):

**(ii) full pipeline (SFT + RL phases + science stage + final run)**

| size | low | central | high |
|---|---|---|---|
| 270M (recorded + unrecorded range) | 130 h ($155) | 136 h ($162) | 157 h ($187) |
| 1B | 222 h ($264) | 350 h ($417) | 568 h ($675) |
| 4B | 587 h ($699) | 1,315 h ($1,565) | 2,871 h ($3,417) |

## Open points
- Fertility and swap numbers are token counts on FLORES+ devtest, not quality; whether more tokens per Ge'ez word explains any share of the amh/tir gap to NLLB is still open and is exactly what the Step 1 arms test (an amh vocabulary of 3,000 tokens would give the model NLLB-like sequence lengths, so a swap arm A5 could be added).
- The 77-language "never needed" sets assume the Latin script is kept whole; 12,939 non-ASCII Latin tokens (for example Vietnamese, Polish) were not examined for removal.
- The HF blog is a secondary source for "English only" of the 1B; no Google statement was found either way.
