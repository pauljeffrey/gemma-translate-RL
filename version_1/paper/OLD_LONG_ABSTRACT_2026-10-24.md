Small but Sabi: Reward-Hardened Reinforcement Learning for a 270M-Parameter Translator into 77 African Languages

**Abstract.** We study how far reinforcement learning (RL) with learned quality metrics can improve a very small model, Gemma 3 270M, on English-to-African translation, starting from a supervised model (SFT) for 77 target languages. Our reward combines a quality-estimation ensemble that takes the pessimistic of two judges when they disagree, a consensus signal for the 23 languages that no metric covers, language-aware sentence alignment that charges omissions, hard failure classes, and constraint detectors calibrated on human text. With GRPO and a length curriculum up to 2,048-token inputs, macro chrF++ on FLORES+ rises from 28.7 to 34.1 (+5.4, 95% CI [5.2, 5.6]; 45 of 45 languages improve) and by 6.6 on rows whose English was never seen in training, with the largest gains where the supervised model was weakest, including languages without any learned metric. Sentence by sentence, against human references, the RL model is preferred by chrF++ in 63.7% and by reference-based AfriCOMET and SSA-COMET in 70.0% and 70.4% of the sentences on which the two models differ (65 of 66 languages better by chrF++, none worse; SSA-COMET, which is not in the reward, agrees with the reward-family metric); this is metric agreement with human references, a benchmark stand-in for a human evaluation that we have not done. The model stays 3.9 chrF++ behind NLLB-200 3.3B on published numbers and, once contamination is controlled, level with or slightly below published 8-14B LLMs. Re-scored on identical FLORES+ sentences with one metric stack (AfriCOMET is published for almost none of the baselines), it is 1.7 chrF++ and 0.035 AfriCOMET behind NLLB-200-distilled-600M and 3.7 and 0.050 behind NLLB-200-3.3B, and ahead of the 600M model in 17 of 44 languages; we do not beat NLLB. It is an open 270M-parameter model fine-tuned with RL against an African-language quality estimator on prompts of up to 2,048 tokens, covering 77 African target languages (55 for NLLB-200-600M); we make no claim to be the first or the only system with this breadth (MADLAD-400 lists about 85 African languages). The document result is narrower than the goal, however. For the RL model, single-pass translation extends from about 256 to about 512 source tokens, and translating sentence by sentence with the same model is what reaches 52 chrF++ against human references at 2,048 tokens. After the supervised science stage the model does finish whole documents of 1,000-2,000 tokens natively (chrF++ 39.4-39.6 at about 1,024 and 28.2-28.8 at about 2,048 tokens against human references, where NLLB-200 600M and 3.3B and TranslatePsy-AfriSLM score 5.9-14.9 in whole-document mode; on AfriScience-MT test papers 41.5 against 8.4-17.9; the step-250 checkpoint finishes 94-100% of the documents and the final checkpoint 91-100% with the same chrF++ (27.7 at about 2,048 tokens, 41.3 on the test papers), its starting model 72-80% at about 2,048 tokens), but it omits 13-30% of the sentences, and with a sentence-by-sentence wrapper every system scores 52.6-58.7 and NLLB and TranslatePsy are 1-4 chrF++ ahead of ours: better on native long input is not better document translation, and we do not claim better document translation than other systems. On the deployment side, GGUF Q8_0 on four CPU cores of a shared server reproduces the chrF++ of the GPU output (-0.02 [-0.22, +0.16]) at about two sentences per second (a laptop speed of 1-2 sentences per second is an estimate, not a measurement); because the Gemma tokenizer needs more output tokens for some languages, Yoruba and Tigrinya sentences take about 1.6 times as long as Swahili ones, and the languages that cost most are also those with the lowest chrF++. We raise, without establishing it, the research question whether RL is a data-efficient complement to SFT: on top of a 3.6-billion-token SFT, RL improved translation against human references and against human-judgement-correlated metrics without additional parallel data (about +4 chrF++ for Amharic and Tigrinya), whereas additional parallel data in the amounts we could add moved the same languages by at most +0.6; confounds (RL ran after a strong SFT, reward and metric overlap, a plateau after about a million completions, a remaining 11 to 19 chrF++ gap to NLLB-200 3.3B for the two languages) prevent a conclusion from these runs alone. We therefore ran a small controlled ablation with a pre-registered decision rule (four languages, 49 RL steps, one seed per arm; RL from a re-created weak SFT with 12.7% of the SFT tokens against RL from SFT-final, and two continued-SFT controls from the weak start, which use our own commercially clean SFT data only). From the weak start RL gained +7.36 chrF++ [6.80, 7.85] on FLORES+ (15.16 to 22.52), against +5.72 [5.15, 6.30] from SFT-final (28.77 to 34.49): the pre-registered rule reads "support" for the gain surviving a weak start, and RL beat both continued-SFT controls from that start (+0.32 [-0.18, 0.82] for continued SFT of equal GPU time, -1.01 [-1.49, -0.55] for equal number of sentences; RL minus control +7.04 [6.49, 7.54] and +8.37 [7.84, 8.83]). It does not show that RL replaces SFT: the weak-start RL model stays 6.25 chrF++ [5.66, 6.87] below SFT-final, and on the sentences that every system finished the chrF++ gain from the weak start is smaller than from the strong start (+1.13 against +2.23; difference -1.10 [-1.60, -0.58]), while AfriCOMET and SSA-COMET with reference give it a larger gain, so much of the weak-start chrF++ gain is repair of unfinished or looping outputs. We document the failures along the way (a diverged phase, a detector length bug, a teacher that could not teach, forgetting in a science stage) and three case studies (Ge'ez script, weak consensus-only languages, an LLM-generated Urhobo silver set). A final run of 401 RL steps on science and general text, started from the science-stage model (interrupted twice by out-of-memory kills, wound down after step 350 by a pre-set stopping rule), moved the sentence-level development scores by at most +0.4 chrF++ (AfriScience dev sentences 48.79 to 48.92) and left 97% of the development papers finished in a single pass against 93% at its start: we read it as a stable continuation, not as a further gain. Evaluated afterwards, its final checkpoint is level with RL-final on FLORES+, NTREX-128, MAFAND-MT and SmolSent (+0.16 to +0.23 chrF++, but 0.001-0.005 lower AfriCOMET and SSA-COMET with reference, which prefer it in 47-48% of the non-tied sentences against 52% for chrF++; one run), and on the AfriScience-MT test split it is +2.4 sentence chrF++ and +13 paper-level chrF above RL-final but only +0.2 and within noise above the science-stage model it started from, so we keep RL-final as the headline model.
