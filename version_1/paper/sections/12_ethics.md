## 12 Ethics, licences and broader impact

**Intended benefit and risk.** A 270M-parameter translator that is small enough to run on a CPU (measured on four cores of a server, laptop speed estimated, Section 6.11) and covers 77 languages could widen access to English-language information (including science, agriculture and health) for speakers of languages that large systems cover poorly. The same property makes misuse and over-reliance cheap: for the weak languages the output is frequently wrong (group means of about 15-27 chrF++ on FLORES+, no human evaluation), and machine translation errors in health, legal or safety contexts can cause harm. We recommend that the model be used only with a native-speaker review in such settings, and we publish per-language scores so that users can see where it fails. Dropped sentences, copied English, padding and loops are documented failure modes of the system and of the reward (Sections 6.4 and 7).

**Community and data provenance.** The work uses data produced by African research communities (Masakhane, SALT, OLDI, MAFAND-MT, IbomMT, AfriScience-MT and others) and the annotation work of their translators. We did not involve native-speaker reviewers in the evaluation or in checking the instruction wording of low-resource languages, which we regard as a gap relative to the participatory practice that these communities set [@nekoto2020participatory]; native review is planned (TODO(owner: confirm plan and funding)). A substantial part of the SFT text for the low-resource languages is religious text of a single publisher, so the model's register is skewed; of 292 scanned repositories 213 had no stated licence and were used with a flag, a few non-commercial sets were used at the project lead's instruction, and publisher copyright for the Jehovah's Witnesses text is not cleared (`reports/weak_language_data_2026-10-22.md` section 5). Synthetic data (NLLB teacher output, Gemini silver set, Gemma-synthetic text) never entered the released-model path except where stated; we used it as a check or as a reward-only signal, and we note the model-collapse risk of training on generated text [@shumailov2023curse].

**Licences.** The weights derive from `gemma-3-270m-it` and are bound by the Gemma Terms of Use [@gemma_terms]. The final run uses NLLB-200 outputs (CC-BY-NC-4.0 weights) as pseudo-references in the reward for five languages [@nllb200_card]; teacher data from NLLB were tested in an SFT mixture (C3) but not adopted. The SFT corpus contains many sources of unstated licence (213 of 292 scanned repositories, used with a flag), Jehovah's Witnesses text whose publisher copyright is not cleared and two owner-approved non-commercial exceptions recorded in the data audit (`reports/weak_language_data_2026-10-22.md` section 5); the project lead reports a separate verification that found no non-commercial data in the SFT mixture, which is not in the repository (TODO(owner: attach the licence verification)). The project lead's position (2026-10-24) is to release the weights under the Gemma Terms of Use plus an open-output addendum (draft: `licenses/SABIYARN_OPEN_OUTPUT_ADDENDUM_DRAFT_v0.1.md`) that allows commercial use and requires publishing data generated with the model, including for the final checkpoint whose RL used NLLB output only as a reward signal. This position has not been reviewed by counsel and is not a legal conclusion (TODO(owner: licence of the model and of the derived data; legal review)). The independent LLM judge of Section 6.12 sent benchmark sentences (SSA-MTE CC-BY-SA, AfriMTE CC-BY, FLORES+ CC-BY-SA, AfriLongDoc) and the existing outputs of NLLB-200 3.3B and TranslatePsy-AfriSLM-0.8B to the Gemini API for scoring, as evaluation baselines only: none of it was used for training, no TranslatePsy data entered any experiment, and judge outputs are used for evaluation only. The code is under AGPL-3.0-or-later (file headers); the AfriLongDoc English side mixes licences by document (relaxed licences, share-alike and non-commercial exceptions, per-record `licence_class`).

**Environmental and financial cost.** The whole programme used one rented GPU at about 1.19 USD per hour; phase 2c alone took 40.5 hours (about 48 USD) and the final run 26.2 hours of step time (28.4 hours of wall-clock, because of a restart at step 150 and two OOM kills); the controlled ablation was budgeted at at most 9 USD including a margin (the cost model gave 5.5 to 7.8 USD) and its chain ran 9.7 hours of wall-clock, about 11.5 USD at the same rate if all of it was billed (about 2 hours of GPU idle after a configuration error and re-runs of three evaluations, Section 8.4). We did not measure energy.
