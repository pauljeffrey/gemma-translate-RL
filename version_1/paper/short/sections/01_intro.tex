\section{Introduction}
% Long paper: Section 1 and Section 11
Most African languages are served poorly by machine translation, and there is demand for models that run without a network on a laptop or phone.
We ask how far a \emph{very small} model (Gemma~3, 270M parameters, of which 168M are the embedding table) can be pushed on English-to-African translation into 77 target languages when the training signal after SFT comes from reinforcement learning (RL) with learned quality metrics instead of more parallel data.
The setting is demanding: the languages range from Swahili and Zulu to Esan, Edo and Okpe, for which the only parallel text we found is largely religious; 23 of the 77 have no learned translation-quality metric at all; and the cheap exploits of a learned reward are well known (copying the source, repeating, appending text, answering in the wrong language, stopping early).
Most of this paper is therefore about what must surround the reward for it to be usable, and about the measurements behind each safeguard; for the extra terms of the final run we find no demonstrated protective effect (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).

\paragraph{Claim.} RL with a reward surrounded by hard failure classes and calibration on human text is a cheap complement to SFT for a very small multilingual translation model: it repairs failure modes and improves translation without extra parallel data, and a controlled ablation shows that the gain survives a weak SFT start and beats continued SFT at equal cost. We do not claim that RL replaces SFT, that the model beats NLLB-200, or any human-judged quality.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \emph{A reward design and its evidence} (Sections~\ref{sec:method}, \ref{sec:analysis}): a quality-estimation (QE) ensemble that takes the pessimistic judge when two judges disagree, per-language shaping on human anchors, language-aware sentence alignment that charges omissions, a consensus signal for languages without a metric, hard failure classes with GlotLID, and constraint detectors whose false-positive rates we measured on human text. We report the measurement behind each component, including negative ones.
\item \emph{Gains over SFT on 77 languages} (Section~\ref{sec:sftrl}): +5.42 chrF++ macro on FLORES+ ($\ci{5.22}{5.61}$; 45 of 45 languages up) and gains on seven further benchmarks, largest for the weakest languages (Kabuverdianu +14.2, Kabiye +14.1), including languages whose reward had no learned metric; we separate the share that is repair of unfinished output. The gain is also positive on the never-seen rows of all eight benchmarks (+6.62 on FLORES+), while our levels are inflated by the exposure of the test sets to our training and reward calibration (Section~\ref{sec:setup}, Appendix~\ref{app:exposure}).
\item \emph{A controlled ablation with a pre-registered rule} (Section~\ref{sec:ab2}): RL from a weak SFT start (12.7\% of the SFT tokens), RL from SFT-final, and two continued-SFT controls; what it shows and what it cannot show.
\item \emph{An honest like-for-like comparison} (Sections~\ref{sec:cmp}, \ref{sec:final}): NLLB-200 and TranslatePsy-AfriSLM re-scored on identical sentences as reference baselines; the model does not beat them (3.32 chrF++ behind NLLB-200 3.3B over 42 languages, 3.85 over all 44, the two Ge'ez-script languages reported alongside; medians 2.78 and 3.17, because a few languages, Kabuverdianu first, carry the gap); native whole-document translation works only after a supervised science stage, omits sentences, and is scored far below the sentence-by-sentence wrapper by an independent LLM judge.
\item \emph{Deployment cost} (Section~\ref{sec:cpu}): quantised to GGUF Q8\_0 the model matches the GPU output on four CPU cores, and the Gemma tokenizer makes some languages cost about 1.6 times as much as others.
\end{enumerate}
\paragraph{Scope and caveats, up front.} There is no human or native-speaker evaluation. AfriCOMET-QE was the reward model and is never presented as independent evidence. Each RL configuration was run once. The test sets overlap our SFT text and, for Efik and the final run, our reward calibration; a fresh evaluation is future work (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). Data licensing of the SFT corpus is not yet cleared. Section~\ref{sec:limits} lists the limitations in full.
