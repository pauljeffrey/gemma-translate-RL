\section{Analysis}\label{sec:analysis}
% Long paper: Sections 7.1-7.3, 6.9; ledger B118-B124, B135, E-part
\paragraph{Reward hacking, and why ``mean or min''.} SSA-COMET-MTL is the better discriminator of wrong-content translations (AUC human against mismatched reference 0.983 against 0.866 for AfriCOMET-QE) but encodes source and translation as one sequence, so an exact copy of the English source scores at least as high as the human reference in 41\% of sentences (AfriCOMET-QE: 1.3\%), and a copy with 20\% of the words dropped in 38\% (4.5\%). Existing guards remove most of this, but a policy under RL could search for the residual (our runs show no copying, below). Averaging the two shaped rewards cuts the copy failure to 8.8\%; the \emph{mean-or-min} rule with $\delta=1$ (the smallest threshold for which the min branch takes at most 15\% of policy chunks; it takes 14.0\%) brings it to 0.8\% for exact and 3.3\% for noisy copies, as low as AfriCOMET alone, while keeping about half of SSA-COMET's gain on mismatched references. The price is that the branch also fires on 22.5\% of human references (they get the lower of two high scores). Appending the source to a correct translation remains rewarded by every metric and is handled only by length and extra-output rules; we did \emph{not} run the A/B of ensemble against AfriCOMET alone.
Other measurements shaped the design: the consensus signal has an attractor (identical attack texts beat honest group members in 95--100\% of simulated prompts without anchors); our first padding detector had a length bug (Appendix~\ref{app:reward}); and the final model really loops in the weak languages (repetition flagged in 30\% of greedy outputs for the 45 weakest languages against 2.1\% of human references).

\paragraph{What the extra hardening did and did not do.} ``Hardened'' describes the design of the final run (ensemble, anchors, long-document reward), not a measured effect. On stored outputs (same detectors, no new generation) RL-final did not show the failures the copy terms target: source copies are at most 0.03\% of its training completions and 0.08\% of benchmark rows, as for the final checkpoint, which is level with it on copying, entity loss and the stop-word profile and loses 1.6 pp more names. Padding and repetition flags are 2--4 pp lower only in the weak and consensus-only languages, and the final checkpoint leaves 40.6 pp fewer 2,048-token documents unfinished than RL-final, 29.3 pp of it already with the science stage; severe repetition rose by 6.9 pp [2.8, 11.3] from step 250 to the end of the run. The ensemble's min branch fires on 56\% of real source copies but at the base rate on padded and repetitive rows. With no control run (same start, plain reward) we attribute none of this to the hardening.

\paragraph{Overlap of metrics with the reward.} Independence has to be tracked (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). For RL-final, chrF++ and SSA-COMET against human references are independent of the reward; AfriCOMET with reference is not strictly independent; AfriCOMET-QE is the reward and is used as no evidence. For the final run SSA-COMET-QE is also in the reward, and its differences from RL-final have opposite signs under chrF++ and the learned metrics, which is why we call it level. The metrics agree on a sentence's winner in 64--73\% of cases only.

\paragraph{An independent LLM judge.} As a check outside the reward stack we used Gemini-3.8-Flash as a judge under the SSA-COMET prompt protocol \citep{li2025ssacomet} (five-shot, score only, Batch API, temperature 0; Appendix~\ref{app:judge}). Gemini-2.5-Pro, that paper's judge, was unavailable to our key; Flash was not worse than Gemini-3.1-Pro-preview on the same 300 segments (0.511 against 0.454). On 300 human-rated SSA-MTE/AfriMTE segments of other systems' output its pooled Spearman correlation with the human score is 0.51 with a reference and 0.49 without (SSA-COMET-MTL: 0.54), so we read only language-level or large differences. On 1,570 FLORES+ sentences in eight languages RL-final scores 62.2 against 58.2 for SFT-final (+4.0, 95\% CI 3.2 to 4.9; higher in seven languages, level in Xhosa), in the direction of AfriCOMET (+5.3) and SSA-COMET (+4.4). On aligned units of 1,000--2,000-token documents (reference-free, five languages) the final model's whole-document output scores 25.8 against 67.0 for its sentence-by-sentence wrapper and 85.6 for NLLB-200 3.3B, and 16\% of the sampled sentences get no output. This is not a human evaluation.
