\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
% Long paper: Section 11, Sections 6.7, 8.4, 6.10, 6.11, 12
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{No human or native-speaker evaluation.} All quality statements are chrF++, spBLEU, AfriCOMET, SSA-COMET, LLM-judge or reward-model scores against human references, and ``improvement'' means improvement against those. chrF++ rewards surface overlap; the pairwise comparison counts how often metrics prefer a sentence and is a stand-in, not a replacement. The independent LLM judge (Appendix~\ref{app:judge}) tracks human ratings of other systems' output only at Spearman about 0.5, so it is a further check and not a substitute. Native review is planned and unfunded: \todoowner{confirm plan and funding}.
\item \textbf{Overlap between metrics and reward.} The reward of RL-final used AfriCOMET-QE (the final run also SSA-COMET-QE, and consensus built from chrF++). AfriCOMET and SSA-COMET with reference share training data and encoders with the reward models, so only chrF++ against human references is independent of every learned model of the reward stack.
\item \textbf{Metric coverage is small.} Of the 77 languages, 42 have a QE score validated against human references, 22 have both AfriCOMET and SSA-COMET scores in our evaluation, and 23 have no learned metric; human-annotated quality data exist for only a handful of language pairs, and 19 of the 85 table rows have no human-reference benchmark. For these languages quality was judged by the reward only.
\item \textbf{The ablation is a small proxy.} Four languages, one 49-step b64 RL phase (2.9\% of the completions of the main run), one seed per arm and control, a re-created annealed weak start, one untuned continued-SFT recipe, no Ge'ez or consensus-only language; part of the weak-start chrF++ gain is repair of unfinished output. It does not answer whether RL can replace or substitute for SFT.
\item \textbf{No control for the extra reward hardening.} No run from the same start with the plain reward exists, so lower padding and repetition in weak languages cannot be attributed to the final run's extra terms (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}); benchmark outputs are greedy, training samples were drawn at temperature 1.
\item \textbf{Single runs everywhere.} Every RL configuration was run once; intervals cover sentence sampling only, not language sampling or seeds. The cause of the phase-2b divergence and of the sentence-level plateau after about a million completions were not isolated.
\item \textbf{Exposure of the test sets, and checkpoint selection.} The SFT text contains the English side of 95\% of FLORES+ devtest (and the reference translation for seven languages), the eight test sets rest on five English texts, and the reward was calibrated on test splits (Efik thresholds; the final run's anchors, long-document limits and NLLB-language choice), so levels on \texttt{all} and \nopair{} rows are inflated (about 3.8 chrF++ for SFT and 2.6 for RL on FLORES+), the Efik IbomMT result and the final run's results on six test sets are not independent, and the effect of the calibration was not measured (no re-run). \clean{} has only 48 sentences per language. We did not check what the published systems saw, so cross-paper comparisons carry an unknown bias, and any comparison on \texttt{all} or \nopair{} rows favours us by about 2--4 chrF++ against a system without exposure. The RL prompt audit is exact and 8-gram only. RL-final is the last step of its phase (the dev-best was step 986), but the science-stage mixture and $\alpha$ were chosen on AfriScience dev sets, so those dev scores are not independent; the best-reward checkpoint of the final run was not kept.
\item \textbf{Behind NLLB, and far behind on Ge'ez.} The model is 3.32 chrF++ and 0.043 AfriCOMET behind NLLB-200 3.3B on identical sentences over the 42 languages without Amharic and Tigrinya (3.85 and 0.050 over all 44; the median chrF++ gaps are 2.78 and 3.17, because a few languages, Kabuverdianu first, carry the mean), clearly worse in 30 of 44 languages, behind TranslatePsy-AfriSLM-0.8B on its 19 languages, and 19.0 and 11.0 chrF++ behind NLLB-3.3B on Amharic and Tigrinya. The cause is untested: capacity shared over 77 languages, tokenisation (862 Ethiopic entries in the 262,144-token vocabulary), the amount and quality of Amharic and Tigrinya data, and Gemma's pretraining coverage are candidates, and NLLB's larger size, far larger data and many more language pairs mean the comparison cannot separate them (a matched specialist or larger model is future work). NLLB is decoded with beam 4 and ours greedily.
\item \textbf{Long documents.} Native whole-document translation works only after a supervised science stage, up to about 2,048 tokens, omits about 12--27\% of sentences, is in-domain for the AfriScience columns, and is below NLLB and TranslatePsy with a sentence wrapper (the LLM judge: 25.8 against 67.0 and 85.6 of 100; its whole-document units are blocks of 1.4 sentences); stitched documents test length, not discourse.
\item \textbf{Licence and provenance are not cleared} (Section~\ref{sec:ethics}).
\item \textbf{CPU numbers come from a shared server} (four pinned cores, 120-sentence timing sets, six languages with Tigrinya standing in for Pidgin, the final checkpoint rather than the headline model); the laptop speed is an estimate.
\item \textbf{A fresh evaluation is planned, not done.} Nothing here is measured on text our pipeline never saw, beyond the few, harder \clean{} rows. The audit recommends the SSA-MTE test split (news, about 200 sentences for each of seven languages; its train split is in our SFT text), WMT24++ (Swahili and Zulu only) and a commissioned human-translated set for weak languages; its cost estimate of 7--18 kUSD for ten languages is unsourced (assumed per-word prices). No fresh result exists; results on one are future work.
\item \textbf{Not safe for high-stakes use.} The weak language groups score 15--27 chrF++ on FLORES+; no safety or toxicity evaluation was done.
\end{enumerate}
