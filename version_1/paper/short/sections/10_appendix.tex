\section{Reward: formulas and calibration facts}\label{app:reward}
% Long paper: Appendix C, D, B
\paragraph{Shaping.} With per-language anchors $(t_{\rm fail},t_{\rm mid},t_{\rm great})$ the shaped reward is the piecewise-linear interpolation through $(0,-2)$, $(t_{\rm fail},0)$, $(t_{\rm mid},+1)$, $(t_{\rm great},+2)$ and $(\max(1,t_{\rm great}+0.001),+2.25)$, constant outside. $t_{\rm fail}=\max(\text{floor},P_{90}$ of the score of mismatched human references$)$ (floor 0 for the ensemble), $t_{\rm mid}=\max(P_{50}$ of human references$,\,t_{\rm fail}+0.05)$, $t_{\rm great}=\max(P_{90}$ of human references$,\,t_{\rm mid}+0.05)$; calibration on FLORES+ dev (997 sentences per language; Pidgin: MAFAND dev; Efik: the IbomMT test set, Appendix~\ref{app:exposure}). In the first QE-only calibration the $+0.05$ spacing floor was active for $t_{\rm mid}$ in 21 of 41 languages, so in those languages the band between the failure line and the human median is only 0.05 QE units wide.
\paragraph{Advantage weight.} $w_\ell=\mathrm{clip}((h-0.5)/0.4,0,1)$, where $h$ is the mean accuracy of ranking the human reference above a mismatched and above a half-omitted version of the same source; hand-set 0.3--0.5 for blend and consensus languages without validation.
\paragraph{Ensemble.} $r=\tfrac12(r_1+r_2)$ if $|r_1-r_2|\le1.0$, else $\min(r_1,r_2)$; the min branch takes 14.0\% of policy chunks (12.3\% in QE languages, 19.3\% in blend languages). The joint encoder budget is 508 tokens for source and translation together; longer pairs are split at sentence boundaries and the piece scores averaged by source characters.
\paragraph{Consensus.} For completion $i$ of a group with at least three valid members $V$: $c_i=\frac1{|V|-1}\sum_{j\in V,j\ne i}\mathrm{chrF{+}{+}}(y_i,y_j)$ (sentence level, word order 2, NFC) and $r_i=-2+4.25\,c_i/100$.
\paragraph{Hard failure classes} (reward $-2$, never scored, first match wins): no end token; text after the end token; turn marker before it; missing or wrong language tag; empty; copy of source (Jaccard $\ge0.9$); degenerate (a word repeated more than four times in a row, fewer than 30\% distinct words of at least 8, zlib ratio $<0.18$); character-length ratio outside $[0.2,5]$; GlotLID mismatch (75 of 77 languages; minimum text length 15--120 characters).
\paragraph{Constraint detectors} (every threshold a quantile of human text): repetition (language-specific quantiles, cross-validated false-positive rate at most 4.5\%); boilerplate (five-grams in at least three other prompts' outputs, buffer of 600 outputs); entity loss (numbers enabled in 67 of 74 calibrated languages, names in 54 of 76); substring copy (threshold $\max(0.08,P_{99.5})$ of the human fraction; $P_{99.8}$ with floor 0.30 in version 3); function-word profile (55 of 77 languages, switched off where no out-of-domain held-out text exists, because thresholds fitted on religious text flagged a median 34.6\% of out-of-domain human text); padding by a segmental type/token ratio (version 3; the first, length-sensitive version flagged 5/14/31/81/100\% of human documents of $<100$/100--200/200--400/400--800/$>800$ words and was replaced).
\paragraph{Long-document reward v2} (sources of at least 150 words). Severity $s\in[0,1]$ is the largest of five ramps (word 4-gram repeat share, words in repeated 8-grams, duplicated-sentence characters, repeated blocks, periodic word cycles), each measured as the excess over the English source and ramped from 1.15 times the 99.5th percentile of human documents of the length bucket to 0.5 (0.6 for the 4-gram share); the reward is $r-s(r+2)$. Aggregation: $R=\bar r-\alpha\max(0,(\bar r-\mathrm{CVaR}_{25})-g_0)$ with $g_0$ the human median of $\bar r-\mathrm{CVaR}_{25}$ per length bucket and $\alpha=1$; a coverage term moves the reward towards $-2$ by $\kappa u$, $\kappa=0.5$, $u$ the failed share of the source weight. With anchored alignment the pair F1 against twelve corruptions is 0.89 (legacy aligner 0.66).

\section{LLM judge: protocol, validation and results}\label{app:judge}
% Long paper: Section 6.12, 7.1a, Appendix J; ledger Part R
\paragraph{Protocol.} Judge: gemini-3.8-flash as served on 2026-10-25, with Figure~8 (reference-based) and Figure~9 (reference-free) of \citet{li2025ssacomet}, verbatim except that ``[67]Most'' is written ``[67] Most'' (the long report, Appendix J, prints the prompts); five demonstrations, one per adequacy interval (SSA-MTE train split for amh, hau, kin, yor; other rows of the same AfriMTE-WMT2024 pair, min--max scaled, for swh, ibo, som, xho); score only; temperature 0, default thinking, Batch API, one run per request. None of the 6,190 responses failed to parse. Cost USD 5.42 (pilot with a second judge, USD 1.82). \emph{Why Flash:} Gemini-2.5-Pro, the judge of the SSA-COMET paper, returned ``no longer available to new users'' for our API key (Batch and standard), so we did not run it and the judge here is not a reproduction of theirs. We compared Gemini-3.1-Pro-preview (Google's named successor) with Gemini-3.8-Flash on the same 300 SSA-MTE/AfriMTE segments (one run each): pooled Spearman with the human score 0.511 $\ci{0.41}{0.60}$ for Flash against 0.454 $\ci{0.35}{0.55}$ for 3.1 Pro, difference Pro minus Flash $-0.057$ $\ci{-0.09}{-0.02}$, per-language intervals of the difference including zero except for Hausa and Somali, at about one fifth of the cost (USD 0.29 against 1.53). We therefore used Flash; we do not claim that it is better than 2.5 Pro. Data: SSA-MTE (CC-BY-SA-4.0), AfriMTE-WMT2024 (CC-BY-4.0), FLORES+, AfriLongDoc core documents; existing outputs of NLLB-200 3.3B and TranslatePsy-AfriSLM-0.8B were sent to the API for evaluation only, and no TranslatePsy data was used in any experiment.
\paragraph{Validation (Table~\ref{tab:judgepilot}).} 300 segments of other systems' output (SSA-MTE amh, hau, kin, yor, 50 each; AfriMTE swh, ibo, som, xho, 25 each; seed 2026, five strata of the human z-score). The reference-free prompt is usable for amh, hau, kin, yor, swh and ibo (kin and ibo only just) and not for som and xho, which we do not use; the paired differences to the reference prompt and to SSA-COMET-MTL-QE are not distinguishable from zero.
\paragraph{Sampling and units.} Run 2: up to 200 \nopair{} sentences per language (Hausa 170), the same for both systems, human reference in the prompt. Run 3: LongCtx-MT core documents of about 1,024 and 2,048 tokens (20 + 20 per language; hau, yor, swh, amh, ibo, kin), 100 sampled source sentences per language, outputs aligned to the source sentences with the long-document reward's aligner (omitted or copied sentences have no unit); whole-document units are blocks of 1.4 sentences on average, wrapper and baseline units single sentences, and the QE prompt was validated on sentences only. Of the final model's whole-document sentences in documents flagged as looping or unfinished (17.5\%) the judged mean is 8.9 against 27.3 elsewhere, so loops explain part of the gap, not all; 4.5\% of its units match a shifted block better than their own.

\section{Exposure of the evaluation sets to our training, selection and reward fitting}\label{app:exposure}

A contamination audit of 2026-10-26 traced where the evaluation text entered our pipeline, separating training signal, selection or monitoring signal and reported evaluation. Table~\ref{tab:exposure} gives the stages with their evidence labels (VERIFIED: read from a scan output, configuration, script or the Hub; UNKNOWN: no file settles it). Three points need stating plainly. (i) The SFT text held FLORES+ sentence by sentence: the English side of 964 of 1,012 devtest sentences, and the translation itself in at least one language for 933; a sample of 87,498 SFT windows shows the English entering through single-sentence Luganda windows (independent translations) and through Igbo, Yoruba, Hausa and Zulu windows, some with the FLORES+ translation, which is why 95.3\% of the English side but only 4.1\% of the pairs were visible to the scan; one carrier repository is named (\texttt{African-Languages-Lab/multi-open} as repackaged in \texttt{<HF_ORG_LEGACY>/sft-new}), the others are UNKNOWN because the windows were not backed up.
