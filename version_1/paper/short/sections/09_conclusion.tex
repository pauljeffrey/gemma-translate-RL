\section{Conclusion}
RL with a reward surrounded by hard failure classes and calibration on human text is a cheap, additional lever for a very small translation model after SFT. It improved a 270M model on every human-reference benchmark we tried (+5.42 chrF++ on FLORES+, 45 of 45 languages up) without extra parallel data, most for the languages where SFT was weakest, and a controlled four-language ablation found that the gain survives a weak SFT start and exceeds continued SFT of equal GPU time. It does not show that RL replaces SFT, a large part of the gain is repair of unfinished output, the model stays behind NLLB-200 and far behind on Ge'ez script, and none of this has been checked by native speakers. The reward carries most of the engineering: shaping on human anchors, language-aware alignment and a consensus signal for languages without a metric, and in the final run a pessimistic judge ensemble and detectors calibrated on human text; we could not show that these extra terms protected against anything (no control run). The test sets overlap our SFT text and, for Efik and the final run, our reward calibration (Appendix~\ref{app:exposure}): the conclusions rest on never-seen rows and on differences between models. The next steps are a fresh evaluation on unseen text, human evaluation, multiple seeds, a larger ablation including Ge'ez languages, and clearing the data licences.
