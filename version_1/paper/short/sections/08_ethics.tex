\section{Ethics and release}\label{sec:ethics}
% Long paper: Sections 12, 13; reports/hf_release_2026-10-24_plan.md
\paragraph{Benefit and risk.} A 270M translator that runs on a CPU and covers 77 languages could widen access to English-language information for speakers of languages that large systems serve poorly. The same property makes over-reliance cheap: output for the weak languages is frequently wrong, and translation errors in health, legal or safety settings can cause harm. We recommend native-speaker review in such settings and publish per-language scores.
\paragraph{Data and licences.} The weights derive from \texttt{gemma-3-270m-it} and are bound by the Gemma Terms of Use \citep{gemma_terms}. The SFT corpus is assembled from third-party datasets with mixed or unstated licences, Jehovah's Witnesses text with uncleared publisher copyright and two owner-approved non-commercial exceptions (Section~\ref{sec:setup}); the final run uses NLLB-200 (CC-BY-NC) output as a reward-side pseudo-reference for five languages; NLLB and TranslatePsy models serve as baselines only. The project lead's draft position is to release under the Gemma Terms plus an open-output addendum (draft v0.1, status: draft, not reviewed by counsel, not a legal conclusion): \todoowner{licence of the model and of derived data; legal review}.
\paragraph{Release state (2026-10-24).} Everything is in \emph{private} Hugging Face repositories: RL-final, SFT-final, the final-run checkpoint, its GGUF Q8\_0 build, the per-sentence benchmark translations with scores, and an archive of RL samples. Nothing is public yet. Planned releases: the weights, the list of data sources with licence classes (not the texts), the code, and the English side of the AfriLongDoc benchmark with per-record licences; the dataset cards carry empty correction columns and a guide so that native speakers can contribute corrections.
