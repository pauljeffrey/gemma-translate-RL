\paragraph{GRPO as used.} For a prompt with target language $\ell$ the policy samples a group of $G=16$ completions at temperature 1 (the sampling distribution is the scored distribution); each receives a reward $r_i\in[-2,2.25]$. With one on-policy update per batch the importance ratio is exactly one. The advantage is group-standardised and weighted by a per-language reliability weight $w_\ell\in[0,1]$,
$A_i=w_\ell\,(r_i-\bar r)/(\sigma_r+10^{-4})$, with $A_i=0$ if $\sigma_r<10^{-6}$ \citep{shao2024deepseekmath}. The loss is the token-level policy gradient plus $\beta\,\mathrm{KL}$ to a frozen reference with a clamped $k_3$ estimator ($\beta=0.05$; 0.1 in the final run); AdamW, learning rate $2\times10^{-6}$ in phase~2c. Prompts are windows of consecutive English sentences (Wikipedia, FineWeb-Edu) in five length bins up to 2,048 source tokens, with draw weights that favour the weakest languages. The \emph{length curriculum} trains the bins in order (b64, b256, b512, b1024, b2048, then a mixture) with the \emph{chunked} reward below, so a long output is scored sentence by sentence although generated in one pass. Phase~2c ran 1,433 steps (about 900,000 sampled completions) on one rented GPU in 40.5 hours, about 48~USD at the quoted 1.19~USD per hour (rate unconfirmed: \todoowner{confirm the hourly rate against the invoice}).

\paragraph{The reward.} The reward is computed per sentence chunk and averaged with source-length weights (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}B). ``Hardened'' below describes the design of the final run; its measured effect is in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.
\emph{(i) Hard failures} receive $-2$ and are never scored: no end token or text after it, a turn marker, a missing or wrong language tag, an empty completion, a copy of the source (word-set Jaccard $\ge0.9$), degenerate repetition, a character-length ratio outside $[0.2,5]$, or another language according to GlotLID \citep{kargaran2023glotlid} (enabled for 75 of 77 languages, those with recall $\ge0.90$ on human text).
\emph{(ii) Chunking:} source and output are aligned by a language-aware Gale-Church aligner \citep{gale1993program} with constant-cost omission and extra-sentence beads; an omitted or extra sentence is a failed chunk ($-2$). Without the language-aware cost the legacy aligner found 0\% of dropped sentences; with it, 56\% (89\% of dropped characters) at 0.6\% false alarms.
\emph{(iii) Content signal.} For the 42 validated and 12 blend languages the content signal is reference-free QE. RL-final used AfriCOMET-QE alone. The final run uses an \emph{ensemble} with SSA-COMET-MTL run without reference \citep{li2025ssacomet}: the two scores are shaped separately to rewards $r_1,r_2$ and combined by the \emph{mean-or-min} rule
$r=\tfrac12(r_1+r_2)$ if $|r_1-r_2|\le\delta$, else $\min(r_1,r_2)$, with $\delta=1$ reward unit, so a chunk that only one judge likes (the signature of copying the source) earns the lower score (tested on corrupted references; effect on the policy not measured). For the 23 consensus-only languages, and with weight 0.5 for the 12 blend languages, the signal is the \emph{consensus}: the mean sentence chrF++ of a completion against the other valid completions of its own group, mapped linearly to the reward range.
\emph{(iv) Shaping.} A raw score is mapped per language onto a piecewise-linear monotone curve with three anchors measured on human references: 0 at the 90th percentile of the score of \emph{mismatched} references, $+1$ at the median and $+2$ at the 90th percentile of matched references, and only $+0.25$ more up to a score of 1 (a score above what humans get is where a learned metric is gamed).
\emph{(v) Penalties and anchors} (penalties only, never bonuses, because a bonus for keeping numbers or names would push towards copying; every threshold is a quantile of human text): length window and word ratio, numbers and names that disappear, diacritic density, and, in the final run, anchors for substring copy of the source, function-word profile, padding by type/token ratio, repetition and a cross-prompt boilerplate buffer. For sources of 150 words or more the final run uses a long-document reward with a continuous repetition severity instead of the length-sensitive degeneracy rule (Appendix~\ref{app:reward}).

\paragraph{Stability guards.} Phase~2b (no division by the group standard deviation, lr $3\times10^{-6}$) diverged when the b256 stage began (KL to the reference 0.04 to 2.6, finished rate 0.95 to 0.56), and dev chrF++ noticed last. Phase~2c restarted from the step-488 model with standardised advantages, a lower learning rate, a KL guard that halves the learning rate when the KL of the last five steps exceeds 0.15 (their median from the b2048 stage on), and a clamp of the log-ratio at 5 in the KL estimator; we did not isolate which change fixed the instability. In the final run the greedy group member's advantage is computed from the sampled members only and scaled by 0.3, because a looping greedy completion of thousands of tokens otherwise dominates the gradient (gradient norm 8--19 against 0.5).
