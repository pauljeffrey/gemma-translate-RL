\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}
% Long paper: Sections 3.1-3.6, 5
\paragraph{Model, task, languages.} The model is \texttt{gemma-3-270m-it} (18 layers, hidden size 640, 262k-entry vocabulary) \citep{gemmateam2025gemma3,google2025gemma270m}, fully fine-tuned. One fixed prompt (\texttt{Translate the text to \{language\}:} followed by the English text) and one answer format (\texttt{\{iso\} \{translation\}}, the three-letter language tag first) are used; a missing or wrong tag is a hard failure in the reward. Only English sources are used. There are 77 target languages, split by the content signal available to the reward: 42 languages whose AfriCOMET-QE score was validated against human references, 12 named by AfriCOMET but not validated (QE blended with a consensus signal), and 23 that no learned metric covers (consensus signal plus constraint penalties). Of these, 66 of the 85 rows of our language table have at least one human-reference benchmark (45 on FLORES+), so for 13 RL languages quality was judged only by the reward models.

\paragraph{SFT data.} All parallel data are existing public datasets, regrouped into windows of consecutive sentences in four length bins (up to about 1,000 tokens), packed with block-diagonal attention; pairs were vetted with AfriCOMET-QE where available. SFT used AdamW then Muon with a UniMax-style language budget and ended at step 27,638 (3.62 B training tokens; macro validation loss 1.2654). \emph{Data-licence caveat:} the corpus is dominated by religious text for the low-resource languages (Jehovah's Witnesses- and Bible-style text is about 58\% of the replay tokens and 75--93\% for most of the weakest languages), 213 of 292 scanned repositories had no stated licence and were used with a flag, the publisher copyright of the Jehovah's Witnesses text is not cleared, and the audit records two owner-approved non-commercial exceptions. A separate verification reported by the project lead is not attached: \todoowner{attach the licence verification}. NLLB-200 and TranslatePsy models are \emph{reference baselines only}; no TranslatePsy data or output enters any training set, reward or reference. NLLB-200 3.3B translations are used as a reward-side pseudo-reference for five languages (Kabuverdianu, Kabiye, Nuer, Bambara, Kabyle; weight 0.23--0.35) in the final run only (Section~\ref{sec:final}), never in SFT, in RL-final or in the ablation.

\paragraph{Evaluation sets, and how much of them our pipeline had seen.} We evaluate English-to-X on eight human-reference \emph{test} benchmarks: FLORES+ devtest (45 of our languages) \citep{oldi2025floresplus}, NTREX-128 (24), MAFAND-MT (15) \citep{adelani2022mafand}, SmolSent (45) \citep{caswell2025smol}, SALT (5) \citep{akera2022salt}, GlotStoryBook (8), IbomMT (2) and FLORES-MOZ (1). They rest on \emph{five} English texts: FLORES+ devtest, IbomMT and FLORES-MOZ share the same 1,012 sentences. A contamination audit (Appendix~\ref{app:exposure}, Table~\ref{tab:exposure}) found that the SFT text contains the English side of 95.3\% of the FLORES+ devtest sentences (SmolSent 91.2\%, SALT 84.4\%, NTREX-128 39.9\%, MAFAND-MT 21.9\%, GlotStoryBook 7.2\%) and, for some languages, the reference translation itself (FLORES+: Hausa 83\%, Igbo 55\%, Yoruba 35\% of the sentences, 933 of 1,012 in at least one of seven languages; MAFAND-MT Igbo 90\%; SALT Luganda 84\%; SmolSent Wolof 73\%); most source repositories could not be traced because the SFT windows were not kept. Every number is therefore reported on three subsets: \texttt{all}; \nopair{} (reference pair not in the SFT text; 96\% of FLORES+ rows; our headline), which removes the pair leak but not the English-side exposure; and \clean{} (English never seen; 48 FLORES+ sentences per language, noisy), the only subset without known exposure. Levels on \texttt{all} and \nopair{} rows are inflated (FLORES+ macro of SFT-final 28.9 on all rows against 25.1 on \clean{}; RL-final 34.3 against 31.7), so we rely on differences between models and on \clean{} rows. No test split was a training target, but test splits were reward-calibration material: the Efik thresholds of RL-final and of the final run (997 of the 1,012 IbomMT sentences), and for the final run its anchors (NTREX-128, SmolSent, SALT, GlotStoryBook, IbomMT, FLORES-MOZ), its long-document limits and the choice of its five NLLB-signal languages from FLORES+ devtest gaps; results on those sets are not independent tests. The RL prompt pools were audited (exact overlap at most 0.7\% of the sentences of any benchmark, 0 of the FLORES+ \clean{} rows; prompts carry no target text; paraphrases not audited), and selection used development sets only (RL-final is the last step). We did not check what the published baselines saw.

\paragraph{Metrics, and which are not independent of the reward.} chrF++ \citep{popovic2017chrfpp} and spBLEU \citep{post2018call} against human references (macro over languages, NFC text, greedy decoding with up to 768 new tokens; the \emph{finished rate} is the share of completions that end inside the budget), and reference-based AfriCOMET-STL-1.1 and SSA-COMET-MTL. Intervals are 95\% paired bootstrap intervals over sentences (1,000 resamples); they contain sentence-sampling noise only, not language sampling or RL-seed variance. \emph{Independence:} the reward of RL-final used AfriCOMET-QE as its only learned metric, so chrF++ and SSA-COMET against human references are independent evidence for RL-final, AfriCOMET with reference shares the reward model's family, and all QE (reference-free) scores are the reward or its sibling; the final run also rewarded SSA-COMET-QE, so for it neither learned family is held out. The consensus part of the reward is built from chrF++ between samples, never against references.

\paragraph{Systems.} \emph{SFT-final} (step 27,638); \emph{RL-final} (GRPO step 1,433 of phase 2c; the headline model); the science-stage model \emph{A2W} and the \emph{final run} (401 further RL steps; Section~\ref{sec:final}); NLLB-200-distilled-600M and NLLB-200 3.3B (CTranslate2, beam 4) and TranslatePsy-AfriSLM-0.8B (zero-shot, its model-card prompt), all re-run by us and scored with the same metric stack on the same sentences, because AfriCOMET is published for almost none of the baselines.
