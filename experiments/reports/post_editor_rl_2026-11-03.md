## Verdict

the greedy reward was flat from about step 20, the held-out (dev) reward went 0.045 at step 20 to 0.016 at step 40, and the KL to E1 rose from 0.046 to 0.067. The only trend that moved was the reward of the T=1 sampled edits (about -0.55 early to about -0.32 at step 52),
together with a rising share of edits identical to the draft (0.13 to 0.24): the policy became more conservative and its sampled edits more like its greedy ones; that is sharpening and copying, not better editing. ## What would be worth trying instead

1. Use E1 only as a repair tool for failed drafts (loops, unfinished, chrF++ < 10), where it gained +3.5 to +6.7 per sentence in the E1 evaluation, gated by a cheap failure detector; no RL needed. This is the one use the evidence supports.
2. If RL is revisited: a much larger effective signal per step is needed. The identity member and dead zone make most groups low-variance; in the 52 steps the greedy reward did not move. Train on drafts that are mostly defective (loops, omissions, wrong language) instead of a 60/20/20 quality mix, so every group has real repairs to learn from; use rejection-sampled SFT (best-of-N edits scored by reference chrF++ and reference COMET on pairs with human references) as a first stage before any GRPO.
3. The reward check showed that QE-neutral edits lose about 2.3 chrF++ on average (rewording that the QE model cannot see); a stronger churn charge or a minimum-edit constraint on reference-free prompts is the first thing to change.
4. Improve the base translator or beam-4 decoding (+1.56 chrF++ over greedy, measured earlier) rather than a second model on top.
