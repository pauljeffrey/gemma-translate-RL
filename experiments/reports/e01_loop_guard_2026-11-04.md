# E-01: does a decode-time loop guard recover the SFT's deficit? (2026-11-04)

Question (paper_385m/EXPERIMENTS_LEFT.md E-01; EVID section 6, option B): about half of the RL-over-SFT gain is "finishing and not looping". Is that a decoding problem that a guard on the SFT fixes without RL? Same harness, rows and metrics as the evidence report (122,585 rows: FLORES+ devtest 54 languages, NTREX 24, MAFAND 15; 214 contamination-flagged rows removed; greedy, 768 new tokens, vLLM bf16), with a decode-time guard in vLLM (`frequency_penalty` / `repetition_penalty` of the sampling parameters, set through `GEMMA_MT_EVAL_FREQ_PENALTY` / `GEMMA_MT_EVAL_REP_PENALTY`). Paired sentence bootstrap as in `reports/rl_vs_sft_evidence_2026-11-04.md`. Data and scripts: `reports/data/e01_loop_guard_2026-11-04/` (`evidence_e01.json`, `guard_dev.json`), `data_pipeline/sft_clean/fresh/overnight/`.

## 0. Short answer

* **The guard recovers about half of the chrF++ gain and under a third of the AfriCOMET gain, and the RL model is still clearly ahead of the guarded SFT.** Macro over the 93 benchmark-language cells, chrF++: SFT 26.59, SFT + guard 30.48 (**+3.89**, 49 % of the +7.96 that RL gives; per-benchmark intervals in section 2), RL 34.55, RL + guard 34.84. Held-out AfriCOMET-STL (76 cells): 0.565, 0.583 (+0.018, 29 % of RL's +0.063), 0.628, 0.631. RL + guard minus SFT + guard: **+4.37 chrF++ [+4.28, +4.46]**, **+0.0479 AfriCOMET [+0.0469, +0.0490]**; on rows where both are finished and loop-free +2.72 [+2.65, +2.79] chrF++ and +0.0345 [+0.0337, +0.0354] AfriCOMET.
* So the finishing share of the gain is about 49 % of chrF++ whichever way it is removed (cutting the SFT's loops after the fact: 49 %, EVID; decoding with a guard: 49 %), and **the paper's thesis stands at "RL adds +4.4 chrF++ and +0.048 AfriCOMET on top of a guarded SFT"**, not at +8.0.
* The guard does no harm to the RL model (+0.29 chrF++ over all cells, +0.41 [+0.38, +0.44] on FLORES+; +0.003 AfriCOMET): it is a decoding fix, not a component of the comparison.
* **Limits:** the guard was chosen by a pre-registered rule on a small dev grid and its optimum was at the grid edge (frequency penalty 0.10), so the control may be under-tuned; section 4 reports a post-hoc extension to stronger penalties. The guarded SFT still fails more than RL: finished 0.9945 (FLORES+) against 1.000, loop flag 13.6 % against 3.0 %, output length 1.41 times the reference against 1.03. One seed; no human evaluation; the loop flag is the RL reward's own guard (see EVID section 6).

## 3. What this changes in the paper

* "About half of the RL gain is finishing" is a decoding fact: it disappears with a one-line sampling-parameter change on the SFT (49 % of chrF++, 29 % of AfriCOMET). The honest claim is RL's gain over a guarded SFT: **+4.4 chrF++, +0.048 AfriCOMET (held out), +2.6 chrF++ on rows where the guarded SFT is clean**.
* AfriCOMET gives the guard less credit (29 %) than chrF++ (49 %): long runaway outputs are punished harder by chrF++ precision than by the neural metric, so the lexical metric overstates the finishing share.
* The remaining differences of the guarded SFT against RL (finished, loop flag, length) are the RL's own contribution to finishing; whether more SFT would also fix them is the open E-03 question.

## 4. Post-hoc extension (stronger penalties) and caveats

The pre-registered grid ended at frequency penalty 0.10, which won. A stronger penalty could close more of the gap. A dev-only extension (frequency penalty 0.20 / 0.30 / 0.50, and 0.20 + repetition 1.02, SFT) is run after seeing these results and is labelled post hoc: see `reports/overnight_log_2026-11-04.md` for its outcome; if a stronger setting wins clearly on dev, a full guarded SFT evaluation with it is the next step. Other caveats: dev chosen on 720 sentences; the guard acts through token frequency and may lower quality for languages that repeat legitimately; the reported loop flag is the RL reward's guard; one seed.
