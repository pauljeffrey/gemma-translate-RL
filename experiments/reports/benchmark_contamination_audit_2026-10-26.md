## Executive summary

1. **SFT: yes.** English side of 95.3% of FLORES+ devtest sentences (98.7% of dev) was in the SFT text, and the FLORES+ translation itself for 7 languages (Hausa 83% of the sentences, Igbo 55%, Yoruba 35%; 933 of 1,012 sentences in at least one language).
2. **Why 95% English but 4% pairs:** a sample of the SFT windows shows the English arriving through single-sentence Luganda windows (independent translation) plus Igbo, Yoruba, Hausa, Zulu windows that carry the FLORES+ translation; one source named (`African-Languages-Lab/multi-open`: Zulu, part of Igbo), the rest UNKNOWN (windows not kept, older repositories return 404).
3. **RL training: no devtest text as a prompt or target** (7 of 1,012 sentences occur in any RL prompt pool, 0 on clean rows).
4. **Two real problems in the reward calibration, not in the paper:** (a) Efik thresholds of RL-final and final2 were fitted on 997 of the 1,012 IbomMT **test** sentences; (b) final2's anchors were fitted on test splits of NTREX, SmolSent, SALT, GlotStoryBook, IbomMT, FLORES-MOZ, its long-document limits on SmolDoc and AfriLongDoc core, and its five NLLB-signal languages were chosen by FLORES+ devtest gaps. Everything else uses FLORES+ dev and MAFAND dev.
5. **Selection and monitoring: dev only** (760-sentence set = FLORES+ dev + MAFAND dev, 0 devtest sentences); RL-final is the last step, not the dev-best; devtest entered only the NLLB language rule, the decoding study, informal draw weights and the headline-model choice.
6. **Size:** FLORES+ levels are inflated by 3.8 chrF++ (SFT) and 2.6 (RL) (all minus clean; about 3.2 and 2.0 after the 0.6 difficulty of clean sentences); SmolSent 6.4 and 4.6; the paper's `no_pair` headline levels stay inflated by about 3.6 and 2.4.
7. **Survives:** RL over SFT is positive on `clean` rows of all 8 benchmarks (FLORES+ +6.62 [5.83, 7.24], NTREX +4.09, MAFAND +3.66, SmolSent +7.06, SALT +2.31 [0.85, 3.57]); NLLB-3.3B stays ahead on identical sentences (-3.84 all, -3.16 clean); kea, kbp, nus, kam, luo gain +12 to +15 on the 48 clean sentences; final2 stays level with RL-final.
8. **Does not survive:** the cross-paper `clean` comparisons of 6.2 and 11 ("6.6", "Afrique turns negative") compare 48 harder sentences with published whole-devtest scores: remove; "published systems did not see these sentences" is unchecked; IbomMT Efik (RL-final) and final2 on six test sets are not independent; the eight benchmarks are five English texts.
9. **Also found:** SSA-MTE train (English + human reference) is in the SFT text; AfriMTE-WMT2024 English is 100% FLORES+ devtest.
10. **WMT offers nothing fresh** (WMT22 African = FLORES-based; WMT24++ only swh, zul; WMT25 only Maasai); recommended fresh evaluation = SSA-MTE test + WMT24++ + a small commissioned set for the weak languages (estimate 7-18 kUSD for 10 languages).
11. **Going forward:** protocol and a tested filter (`contam_audit_12_decontaminate.py`) in section 5; scripts and data in section 6.
12. Labels: VERIFIED (read from a file or the Hub), REPORTED, UNKNOWN (file or scan that would settle it is named).


## 1. SFT: what test-set text was in the supervised training text

### 1.1 The scan that produced the `clean` and `no_pair` flags, and what it can and cannot see

- The flags were made on 2026-10-18/19 by `python -m gemma_mt.eval.contamination --windows gemma/windows_sft ...` (`gemma_mt/gemma_mt/eval/contamination.py`; `meta.created` and `meta.training_windows = 11,442,172` in every `contamination_<benchmark>.json`; `research_archive_2026-10-23/box/other/sft_build/bench/test_sets/_contamination/`). 11,442,172 is the window count of the final SFT build of paper Section 3.4. VERIFIED.
- Definitions (VERIFIED in the module docstring and code): `exact` = the normalised English sentence of the benchmark row equals a normalised sentence of some training window, or occurs inside a window as a contiguous token run (the headline "contaminated"; `clean` = not `exact`); `sentence` = the whole-sentence rule only (the old dev filter); `near` = not exact but at least 50% of the sentence's 8-token n-grams occur in the training English; `pair` = `exact` AND the normalised reference of the row occurs in the target side of a window of the same language (`no_pair` = not `pair`). Normalisation: NFKC, case-fold, every run of non-alphanumerics to one space.
- What the flags cannot see (design, VERIFIED from the code; none of it is quantified anywhere): (i) the key is the English side, so a benchmark target text that was trained on with a *different* English sentence, a French or other pivot, or in an African-to-African pair, is invisible; (ii) `pair` needs an exact substring match after normalisation, so a re-spelled, NFD or differently tone-marked copy of the reference is missed (the paper itself measures 28% of the Yoruba FLORES+ references as not NFC, Section 6.8), hence `pair` is a lower bound; (iii) paraphrase, machine translation or retranslation of the same English; (iv) `near` rows are kept in `clean` (`clean_strict` removes them; FLORES+ has 0 near rows, NTREX 144, MAFAND 101). The scanner reproduced the owner's old dev filter exactly (10,043 of 30,418 dev rows clean, zero differences; `reports/gemma_sft_benchmarks_2026-10-18.md` section 3, REPORTED).

## 2. RL: training signal, selection signal, reported evaluation

Three roles are kept apart throughout: **T** = training signal (enters the loss through prompts, reward, advantage weights or the start model), **S** = selection / monitoring signal (decides which checkpoint, mixture, interpolation weight, language set, stopping point is used), **E** = reported evaluation. Models: SFT-final (step 27,638) -> RL-final (phase 2c, step 1,433, the paper's headline) -> science stage + alpha 0.7 interpolation (A2W) -> final run (final2, step 401).

### 2.1 Role T, part 1: the prompts

## 5. Going forward: a protocol so that no test text enters the new specialist's training, reward or selection

### 5.1 Roles and the rule for each (this is the rule that was broken in 2.2-2.3)

| role | may use | may not use |
|---|---|---|
| T1 training text (SFT, distillation, replay, teacher data, RL prompts, RL-archive translations, pseudo-references) | anything that passes the filter of 5.3 against **every** protected set, on **both** sides, in **any** language | any file of a protected set (dev, devtest, test, validation), any aggregated collection before it was scanned |
| T2 reward calibration and reward references (shaping anchors, length, diacritics, LID, lexicons, anchors, long-document limits) | a **calibration set** fixed in advance (5.4), disjoint from the evaluation sets | any test split, even "for thresholds only" |
| S selection and monitoring (checkpoint, interpolation weight, mixture, language set, stopping, decoding) | a **selection set** fixed in advance (5.4), disjoint from T2 and from the evaluation sets | any set that is later reported |
| E reported evaluation | the sealed sets of 5.4, read after all decisions are logged | nothing from E in T1, T2, S |

### 5.2 Protected sets (English side and every target side)

FLORES+ dev and devtest (all 200+ languages, including the FLORES-200 variants `aka_Latn`, `twi_Latn_asan1239`, `gaz_Latn`), IbomMT (all splits for dev/test; train only after the English scan), FLORES-MOZ, NTREX-128, MAFAND-MT (dev and test, French and English sources), SmolSent, SmolDoc, GATITOS, SALT (dev and test), GlotStoryBook, AfriScience-MT dev and test, AfriLongDoc (core, hard, private), TICO-19, SSA-MTE and AfriMTE-WMT2024 (as long as they serve judge or metric validation), WMT24++ and any fresh set of 5.6, FLORES-derived sets (xsim++, SIB-200, WMT22 African validation/test, xP3/xP3x `flores` files, `AfroBench` MT tasks), plus the benchmarks of the language-understanding family if the specialist is also evaluated on them (XNLI, MGSM/GSM8K, MMLU for AfriXNLI/AfriMGSM/AfriMMLU). The index is built once, versioned, and the list is part of the run's configuration.

### 5.3 Normalisation and matching rules (implemented and self-tested in `gemma_mt/scripts/contam_audit_12_decontaminate.py`)

1. Normalise: NFKC, case-fold, quote look-alikes folded, every run of non-alphanumerics to one space (the rule of `gemma_mt/eval/contamination.py`, so that old and new flags are comparable). In addition a second pass with combining marks stripped (NFD, drop marks): Yoruba, Fon, Ewe, Igbo and others circulate in NFC, NFD and with U+0323 / U+0329 under-dots (28% of the Yoruba FLORES+ references are not NFC), and the current `pair` flag cannot see a re-spelled copy.
2. Match each text of a training pair (the English or pivot side AND the target side, whatever its language) against the English and the target sentences of every protected set: (R1) exact normalised sentence equality, texts of fewer than 3 tokens never match; (R2) the protected sentence occurs inside the text as a whole-token run (windows, concatenations, paragraphs); (R3) at least 50% of the text's word 8-grams occur in the protected set (near duplicate), and for the **English side of the evaluation sets** any shared 8-gram marks the row for review (texts under 8 tokens: exact only); for document sets (SmolDoc, AfriLongDoc, AfriScience) use word 8-grams and token 12-grams as in `longdoc_contam.py`.
3. Drop the whole window or pair on any hit; never "repair" one side. Because the sets are multi-way, a Hausa sentence equal to FLORES+ Hausa is a leak for every direction; the check must therefore use the target side of every protected language, not only the benchmark language of the row.
4. Source-level quarantine before row-level matching: reject any repository whose name or file names contain flores, ntrex, mafand, smol, salt, tico, sib200, xsim, afrobench or whose files are named dev / devtest / test / validation; for aggregated collections (naija-style repositories, `African-Languages-Lab/multi-open`, `<HF_ORG>/*`, xP3/xP3x, Aya, `Inkuba`) use train files only **and** run the row-level scan (the `multi-open` Igbo rows show that "train only" is not enough).
5. Provenance is mandatory: every window keeps its `source_dataset`, file and original split; the decontamination report (rows removed per rule and protected set) is stored next to each build. This audit could not name the carriers of FLORES+ only because `pairs_final` (with `source_dataset`) was not backed up.
6. After the build, run the original scanner over the final windows and store the per-row flag files with the run (they were the only reason this audit could be done); re-run it on every change of data. Plant canaries: a few hundred held-back sentences in the fresh set carry a GUID line in the release so that later models can be tested.
7. Assertions in code: every calibration script refuses a file whose split label is not in {calibration, dev}; the trainer config records the split name of each set used for T2 and S and a checksum of the protected index.
8. RL prompts: filter against the English index before pool building (the v2/v3 pools were not decontaminated; 0.69% of FLORES+ sentences were in them), and against the reward models' own training sets where known (AfriMTE, SSA-MTE train).

### 5.4 Which sets to hold out, and for which role (proposal for the new specialist)

| role | set | why |
|---|---|---|
| E (sealed final) | FLORES+ devtest, NTREX-128, SmolSent, SALT test, GlotStoryBook, MAFAND-MT test, AfriScience-MT test, AfriLongDoc, **plus the fresh set of 5.6** | keep comparability with this paper; report `all`, `clean` and fresh separately; FLORES+/IbomMT/FLORES-MOZ count as one English text |
| S (selection) | FLORES+ **dev** (English removed from T1/T2 as well) and the SSA-MTE dev split; AfriScience dev for science | disjoint from E; FLORES+ dev is in the old SFT text (98.7%) so it can only be used after the new text is decontaminated |
| T2 (calibration) | a pool that is neither S nor E: SSA-MTE **train** references (human, CC-BY-SA, 16 languages; remember that it was in the old SFT text, so it is a calibration set only if SSA-MTE is also removed from the new SFT), FLORES+ dev only if S uses another set, plus pooled priors for languages without human text | the Efik case shows what happens when the only human text of a language is a benchmark: either find a calibration set for it (SmolSent/Gamayun-style kits are not allowed if they are E) or use consensus-only with pooled priors and say so |
| T1 | never any of the above | |

### 5.5 Verification and effort

- Cost of the filter: the scanner indexes 0.9 M protected sentences and scans 11 M windows in minutes on CPU (the 2026-10-18 run took 2 minutes with 8 processes). The new script is pure Python; for tens of millions of pairs use the `first-gram` index as in `contamination.py` (the same structure) or a Rust/Polars hash join.
- A pre-registered contamination table is produced with every checkpoint: rows removed, `all`/`no_pair`/`clean`/fresh scores; the claims of the paper are made on `clean` and fresh rows.

Answer to the owner's question "what about WMT test?": for our 77 languages the WMT tasks supply nothing fresh: the WMT22 African test material is FLORES-based and therefore the very text we have to avoid; WMT24++ has Swahili (two locales) and Zulu; WMT25 has Maasai; NTREX is WMT19 newstest and is already used. The only public, human-referenced, post-2024 data we found for the weaker languages (Kikuyu, Luo, Twi, Kinyarwanda, Amharic, Hausa, Yoruba) with English sources outside FLORES is the SSA-MTE test split (news, about 200 sentences per language); AfriMTE-WMT2024, the other African metric set, uses FLORES+ devtest sentences as its English.

### 5.7 Recommendation: one fresh set built in three layers, with the expected clean share

1. **SSA-MTE test (English to amh, hau, kik, kin, luo, twi, yor; optionally ibo, zul, swh, sot)**: free, CC-BY-SA, a human reference per sentence, news; 204-245 sentences per language. Use it as the *public* leg of the new evaluation. Expected clean share against our old text: RL pools 99.8% (exact) and 99.6% (>= 50% 8-gram); SFT-final: English disjoint from the SSA-MTE train split that we did train on, but 2 of 466 sentences were hit in an 87,498-window sample of the SFT windows, so the exposure is probably between 2% and 30% and UNKNOWN exactly; the **new specialist's text must be filtered against it** (5.3) and the SSA-MTE train split must be removed from the new SFT if the test split is to count as unseen. Caveats: 200 sentences give an interval of about +-2 chrF++ per language; the reference quality of volunteer-translated Global Voices is unknown; SSA-COMET is built on it.
2. **WMT24++ English to Swahili (Kenya, Tanzania) and Zulu**: 998 segments, mixed domains, 2024 English sources; expected clean share 99.8% against the RL pools, unknown (probably high) against the SFT text, and unknown for the Gemma-3 pretraining. It tests the high-resource claims on newer text, covers 2 languages only, and its references are post-edited machine translation (a bias toward Google-style output).
3. **A newly commissioned set for the weak languages, which no public set covers** (kea, kbp, nus, kam, bam, dyu, umb, kmb, mos, tir, orm ... as many as the budget allows): 300-500 English sentences per set, written or collected *after* the data cutoff of the specialist (for example Wikinews articles dated after 2026-11-01 and newly composed sentences by the annotators; checked against the protected index and against a web-n-gram lookup), translated by two independent paid native translators with a reviewer, sealed until all decisions are logged, with canary GUIDs on release. Expected clean share: 100% by construction against all our text; the effort is the translation. Rough effort, **an estimate with no source behind it**: 400 sentences x about 22 words = 8,800 words per language; 10 languages = 88,000 words; at an assumed market price of 0.08-0.20 USD per word this is about 7,000-18,000 USD plus review, 3-6 weeks lead time; a Masakhane-style volunteer model is cheaper and slower. A cheaper pilot is two to three languages (kea, kbp, nus: the paper's highlights) with 300 sentences each.
