**Abstract.** [DRAFT: the experiments the abstract relies on are in (E-01 to E-05, E-09b, E-11, E-13, E-15, E-16, E-20); gates in `PAPER_PLAN.md` section 7.] We grow Gemma-3-270m to a 385M-parameter, 39-layer English-to-African translator with 125 single-token language tags, fine-tune it on decontaminated data (no benchmark sentence of three or more words in the training text) and apply GRPO with reference-free quality-estimation rewards. RL raises FLORES+ devtest chrF++ from 22.85 to 31.66 (+8.81, 95% CI [+8.59, +9.03], 54 languages) and held-out AfriCOMET by +0.063; every FLORES+ language improves. A one-line decode-time repetition guard on the SFT (a vLLM frequency penalty of 0.10, chosen on a dev grid by a rule fixed in advance) recovers 49% of the chrF++ gain and 29% of the AfriCOMET gain without any RL (SFT 26.59 -> 30.48 chrF++; cutting the SFT's loops after the fact gives the same 49%); RL still adds +4.37 [+4.28, +4.46] chrF++ and +0.0479 [+0.0469, +0.0490] AfriCOMET on top of the guarded SFT. Beam-4 and MBR-16 at 768 tokens also help the SFT (+3.71 [+2.78, +4.47] and +5.96 [+5.37, +6.49] chrF++), but RL greedy stays +1.60 [+1.34, +1.88] chrF++ ahead of the best decoded SFT. 94% of the gain up to the second RL stage arrives in the first stage; the B1 step adds +0.54 chrF++ on FLORES+ and the mixed step +0.31, which on the 20 languages of a seed study (two extra seeds differ by 0.06 chrF++ [-0.02, +0.14] after 100 steps) is not separable from seed noise, and RL aimed at eight weak languages adds +0.03. Removing the four features added at step 41 of stage A costs 0.32 chrF++ [0.23, 0.41] at 100 steps (reference domain 0.16, beam guide not separable from seed noise) and leaves the KL trajectory unchanged. On the 43 languages shared with the published NLLB-200 table the final model reaches 35.54 chrF++ against 38.80 for NLLB-3.3B (92%; the SFT 68%). The loop flag that the reward itself uses undercounts degeneration by a factor of 1.77 on FLORES+, at a cost of at most 0.19 chrF++. A science SFT improves surface metrics on AfriScience-MT but not SSA-COMET-QE, whole-document decoding fails and sentence-by-sentence decoding is required, and the first research's 270M model is as good as or better at document level. A science RL adds +0.46 chrF [+0.27, +0.65] on AfriScience test sentences over its start, and the science SFT, not the science RL, carries the science gain (the same RL started without the science SFT ends 1.44 chrF [1.25, 1.61] lower, and the registered rule says the science SFT is needed) and a long-document RL improves whole-document generation modestly (finished 0.70 -> 0.82, loop 0.85 -> 0.73, chrF 27.4 -> 30.9) without changing chunked or sentence-level quality; whole-document decoding stays unusable (72 to 73% loops). A matched-compute control, continued SFT for the GPU time of the RL chain (6,000 steps, 8.4 h) on a 50/50 mix of replay and NLLB-translated TranslatePsy English-to-X pairs for 19 languages, reaches 29.97 chrF++ over the 93 benchmark-language cells against 34.55 for the RL model (RL minus control +4.58 [+4.45, +4.70]; held-out AfriCOMET +0.0183 [+0.0172, +0.0192]). Its pre-registered verdict is "inconclusive", because on the 19 languages that have the teacher-translated corpus RL minus control is -0.03 [-0.13, +0.09] chrF++, while on the other 46 cells it is +9.29 [+9.09, +9.49]; the control repairs finishing and looping only on those 19 languages. All results are single-seed, the control has one learning rate and one evaluated budget point, and no human evaluation was run (Section 13).

## 1 Introduction

[SKELETON, write after the abstract.]

* Problem: a small translator for many African languages fails mostly by not stopping (unfinished, looping output), copying and writing the wrong language; supervised fine-tuning alone leaves these in place (SFT: FLORES+ finished 0.829, loop 0.209, output 3.03 times the reference length; Section 7.2).
* What we do: grow the model, train it on decontaminated data, apply GRPO with reference-free quality-estimation rewards, then try targeted RL, science SFT/RL and long-document RL.
<!-- GATE E-01 E-03 -->
* What we find, in the order of the evidence: R1-R8, R19-R20, N2, W4, SC3, DL1-DL3 of the ledger. The gain is large, about half of it is finishing and looping, most of it arrives in the first RL stage, and what the RL leaves unrepaired (Arabic dialects, Tamazight, Dinka, Tamasheq) it partly worsens (English output).
<!-- GATE E-01 E-03 -->
* What we do not claim: that RL is in general a better use of compute than more SFT (our one matched-compute control has the registered verdict "inconclusive": RL matches it on the 19 languages it was given a teacher corpus for and is far ahead on the other 46 cells, Section 7.2.2); that the finishing gap is a property of RL rather than of greedy decoding (it is not: a decode-time guard recovers 49% of the chrF++ gain, Section 7.2); any single reward component beyond the stage-A feature ablations of Section 8 (the four stage-A features together help by 0.32 chrF++ at 100 steps; the beam guide is not shown to be worth its 25% step time); that the model is competitive with NLLB (the final model is 3.26 chrF++ below NLLB-3.3B on 43 shared languages, with caveats; Section 7.6); that the 385M beats the first research's 270M model; any "first".
<!-- GATE E-01 E-03 -->
* Contributions (to be worded after the controls): (1) a decomposition of an RL gain into finishing/looping and translation quality, with the metric-independence discipline of Section 6.3 and an independent degeneration audit of the reward's own loop flag (Section 7.2); (2) a decontaminated SFT/RL pipeline for 125 languages with an audit of the training text and its follow-ups; (3) negative results: weak-group RL, science SFT on the headline metric, post-editor, whole-document decoding; (4) released artifacts (models public on the Hub, no licence field declared; Section 14.5). The four stage-A features are described as design choices with a small measured benefit (E-11), not as contributions (Section 8).

## 2 Related work

**Machine translation for African languages.** Community infrastructure and benchmarks: the participatory Masakhane work [@nekoto2020participatory], MAFAND-MT [@adelani2022mafand], AfroMT [@reid2021afromt], FLORES-200 and NLLB-200 [@goyal2022flores101; @nllb2022nllb; @nllb2024nature], its community successor FLORES+ [@oldi2025floresplus; @dale2025oldi], NTREX-128 [@federmann2022ntrex], MADLAD-400 [@kudugunta2023madlad], SALT [@akera2022salt], SMOL and GATITOS [@caswell2025smol], and the Bible and Jehovah's Witnesses corpora [@agic2019jw300; @akerman2023ebible] on which much low-resource training data rests. AfroBench evaluates LLMs across 64 languages [@ojo2025afrobench]. Larger African-language LLMs include AfriqueLLM [@yu2026afriquellm] and Sunflower [@akera2025sunflower]; TranslatePsy-AfriSLM [@gritta2026translatepsy] is the nearest small-model competitor; smaller African models include InkubaLM [@tonja2024inkubalm] and a pruned NLLB-600M [@moslem2026afrinllb]. AfriScience-MT [@abdulmumin2026afriscience] provides sentence- and document-level science translation for six languages and reports SSA-COMET [@li2025ssacomet] as its primary metric with chrF [@popovic2015chrf] as a surface check.

**RL for translation.** GRPO [@shao2024deepseekmath] and its analyses [@guo2025deepseekr1; @liu2025understanding; @yu2025dapo] underlie R1-style MT work: MT-R1-Zero [@feng2025mtr1zero], R1-T1 [@he2025r1t1], DeepTrans and ExTrans [@wang2025deeptrans; @wang2025extrans], Seed-X [@cheng2025seedx], Hunyuan-MT [@zheng2025hunyuanmt; @zheng2025hymt15], and TranslateGemma, which uses a reward-model ensemble on Gemma 3 at 4B and up [@finkelstein2026translategemma]. Closest to our setting: RL on NLLB with round-trip chrF++ [@attia2026roundtrip], reference-free GRPO on NLLB [@garciaestrada2026reffree], chrF rewards for unseen languages [@hu2026rlunseen], WALAR's treatment of QE-reward hacks in MT [@liu2026walar] and language-ID-gated two-QE rewards [@han2026milmmtrl]. Label-free signals such as sample agreement [@zuo2025ttrl; @wang2023selfconsistency] correspond to our consensus reward. Minimum Bayes risk and quality-aware decoding [@freitag2022mbr; @eikema2020map; @fernandes2022qualityaware] are the competing route to many of the same gains; we measure beam and MBR on our SFT model (Section 7.5). We searched (2026-11-05) for matched-compute SFT-versus-RL comparisons in MT and found none; the nearest, reference-free GRPO on NLLB compared with three epochs of supervised fine-tuning on 13 languages [@garciaestrada2026reffree], does not match GPU time.

**Metrics and reward hacking.** COMET-family and MetricX metrics [@rei2020comet; @rei2022cometkiwi; @guerreiro2024xcomet; @juraska2024metricx24], AfriCOMET [@wang2024africomet] and SSA-COMET [@li2025ssacomet] for African languages; chrF++ and sacreBLEU [@popovic2017chrfpp; @post2018call]; paired bootstrap [@koehn2004significance]; overoptimisation of learned rewards [@gao2023scaling; @karwowski2023goodhart; @krakovna2020specification; @coste2024ensembles] and length exploits [@singhal2024long]; language identification [@kargaran2023glotlid].

**Small models, growth and forgetting.** Gemma 3 and its 270M variant [@gemmateam2025gemma3; @google2025gemma270m]; the curse of multilinguality [@conneau2020unsupervised; @chang2024multilinguality] and tokenisation [@petrov2023tokenizer]; temperature-based language sampling [@chung2023unimax]; depth growth with identity-initialised blocks [@wu2024llamapro; @kim2023solar]; Muon [@liu2025muon]; weight-space interpolation [@wortsman2022robust]; replay and forgetting [@rolnick2019experience; @shenfeld2025razor].

**Documents.** Document-level translation and its failure modes [@wang2023document; @wu2024adapting; @varis2021sequence; @liu2024lost; @holtzman2020curious; @welleck2020unlikelihood], AfriDoc-MT [@alabi2025afridocmt], sentence alignment [@gale1993program; @thompson2019vecalign], RL for long outputs [@song2025formatrl; @wu2025longwriterzero].

**Recipe.** Samples are packed into 4,096-token packs (one language per pack, block-diagonal attention) at 32 packs = 131,072 tokens per step; each pack is used once. ## 5 Reinforcement learning method

<!-- ledger M7-M10, R9, R10, W6 -->

**GRPO as used.** One on-policy update per sampled batch; a prompt gets a group of 16 completions (15 sampled plus the greedy output; in guided groups 14 sampled, the guide and the greedy output); the advantage is the group-standardised reward; the loss is the token-level policy-gradient term plus a k3 KL penalty to a reference model (coefficient 0.05 in stages A to mixed; the reference is the start model of each stage, i.e. the SFT final for stage A and the previous stage's checkpoint afterwards, because every stage restarts with a fresh reference and a fresh optimiser) [@shao2024deepseekmath; @schulman2020approximating]. Optimiser: Muon (momentum 0.95, 5 Newton-Schulz steps), **learning rate 2e-6** with 10 warm-up steps, weight decay 0, gradient clipping 1.0; temperature 1.0, top-p 1.0; at most 384 new tokens in the short-window bin (b64) and 1,280 in b256 and the mixed stage; seed 1234. A KL guard cuts the learning rate when the median KL exceeds a threshold (0.15 in stage A, 0.25 later). Prompts per step: 64 in stage A, 48 in B1, 42 in mixed. <!-- src: HYG section 3 (M7: runs_385m/{A,B1,mixed}/config.json, copies in reports/data/e23_hygiene_2026-11-04/rl_configs/); repository configs gemma_mt/configs/clean_rerun_385m/rl_A_b64.yaml, rl_B1_b256.yaml, rl_C_mixed.yaml (learning_rate 2.0e-06, kl_coef 0.05, model_name = KL reference AND policy start); reports/rl_stage_A_385m_2026-11-03.md; reports/rl_vs_sft_evidence_2026-11-04.md section 6 -->

**Stages.** Stage A (400 steps, short English windows, bin b64), B1 (135 steps run, b256; best checkpoint step 50 by dev chrF++), mixed (60 steps, b64 and b256), then a continued-RL run on eight weak languages (85 steps), a science SFT with weight interpolation, a science RL and a long-document RL (Section 9). B1 started from stage A's final model (step 400), mixed from B1's best checkpoint (step 50), the weak-group run from the mixed best, the science SFT from the mixed best. The dev set has **720 sentences = 18 FLORES+ dev languages x 40 sentences** (afr ewe fon hau ibo kin nya orm sna som sot swh tsn twi wol xho yor zul). B1's step-135 weights (Hub branch `stage-b1-final`) were evaluated after the fact (E-20, Section 7.3) and equal B1 best within noise. <!-- src: HYG X3, X4, X19; ledger M8. Step 200 of stage A was the dev-best checkpoint at its time (dev 40.4697); the final model of step 400 (dev 40.50, a gain of 0.03 below the selection minimum 0.1) is the one every evaluation uses -->

**Prompts.** English windows of consecutive sentences from FineWeb-Edu and Wikipedia (70%) and from news (BBC, Wikinews, Global Voices, and Wikivoyage; 30%), filtered against the protected index; 81 languages with higher draw weights for the 29 weakest. The stage-A news pool contained Wikivoyage (`darkpredator/stealth-wikivoyage-en`; 188 prompts drawn in steps 1-400) and an unlisted source `SetFit/bbc-news` (37 pool rows, none drawn). <!-- src: reports/rl_prompts_news_2026-11-03.md; reports/rl_stage_A_385m_2026-11-03.md; ledger L5, CT13; CFU section 1 -->

**Reward.** Per completion: hard failures (missing or wrong language tag, unfinished or text after the end token, copy of the source, degenerate repetition, wrong language by GlotLID [@kargaran2023glotlid] with a 15-character minimum, length outside the human window) receive the floor reward -2. Otherwise the content signal is a reference-free quality-estimation ensemble of `masakhane/africomet-qe-stl-1.1` [@wang2024africomet] (r1) and `McGill-NLP/ssa-comet-mtl` run without a reference [@li2025ssacomet] (r2), each shaped per language onto a monotone curve calibrated on human references (held-out calibration split of 261 ids, never dev, devtest or test). **The ensemble is the mean of the two shaped rewards if |r1 - r2| <= 1.0 and the minimum otherwise**; it exists for 42 languages (41 with an ensemble block; Dyula has none). For languages without a validated QE model the content signal is the consensus (mean sentence chrF++ against the other valid group members, at least 3 valid members, blend 0.5); for five weak languages (kea kbp nus bam kab) an NLLB-3.3B pseudo-reference chrF++ term is added with weight 0.25 (max 0.35), reward only. Penalties are penalties only (never a bonus), as executed in stages A, B1 and mixed: length (weight 1.0 per log-unit outside the language's human length-ratio window, widened by 15%), word ratio (0.5 per log-unit of the words ratio above the human 99th percentile, slack 15%), lexical (1.0 per unit of deficit in numbers, names and content words that are not in the source), diacritics (1.0), and the constraint anchors (boilerplate, copy with cap 2.0, entity recall 0.5, function words 0.3, padding, repetition and a lexicon guard; v1 for consensus and blend languages, v2 for all three content modes; a saturated stuffing deficit at 0.9 is a hard failure); the fluency and science-anchor penalties were off; hard-failure classes also include an empty output. The reward range is [-2, 2.25]. The reward is computed per sentence chunk and aggregated by source length, with failed chunks at -2 (long-document reward `longdoc_v2`, applied to short bins too). <!-- src: HYG section 3 (M9: gemma_mt/gemma_mt/rl/qe_ensemble.py header, shaping_clean_v2.json `ens_delta` 1.0, training_state reward_range); reports/rl_vs_sft_evidence_2026-11-04.md section 1 (reward-family table); reports/rl_stage_plan_385m_b256_2026-11-02.md section 3; penalty list read from the reward code and the executed configs (`research_archive_2026-11-03/box_final_2026-11-04/clean_rerun/tree/gemma_mt/gemma_mt/rl/reward.py`; `reports/data/e23_hygiene_2026-11-04/rl_configs/config_{A,B1,mixed}.json`, identical reward block in A, B1 and mixed) -->

**Four features added at step 41 of stage A.** Stage A ran steps 1-40 without them; at the step-40 checkpoint the trainer was stopped and resumed from `last/` with all four switched on. (1) Dynamic sampling: oversample 1.1 (later 1.0), drop flat groups, refill up to twice [@yu2025dapo]; (2) an advantage standard-deviation floor of 0.02; (3) a beam-guided group member: for 25% of the prompts the beam-4 output (or, for 31 weak languages, an MBR winner over 16 extra samples; skipped on 99% of B1 steps for cost) replaces the last sampled member, with ratio 1 and the guides' advantage share capped at 0.25; (4) a reference-anchored term on 15% of the prompts (7 languages: amh efi hau lug nso yor zul, human pairs from AfriScience-MT train and IbomMT train): r = 0.65 r_existing + 0.35 consensus_value(chrF++ against the human reference). In stage A the std floor never bound (group-std p10 0.128 against 0.02) and dynamic sampling never acted (flat fraction 0); the beam guide cost a median 26.6% of step time and was above the mean of the other members in 83% of groups. The stage-A log cannot separate the four features, so we ablated them at 100 steps (E-11, Section 8): together they are worth a small gain (+0.32 chrF++), mostly the reference-anchored term, the beam guide is not shown to be worth its 25% step time, and none of them causes the KL rise; we describe them as design choices, not as contributions. The archived stage-A `config.json` is the resumed (post step 40) configuration, so the values of steps 1-40 are known only from the stage report. <!-- src: reports/rl_features_dynamic_ref_2026-11-03.md; reports/rl_stage_A_385m_2026-11-03.md; HYG X18; SPLIT -->

**Learning rate in stage A.** The KL guard cut the learning rate at steps 226 (x0.5), 236 (x0.25) and 247 (x0.125) and it never recovered: steps 247-400 ran at 2.5e-7 instead of 2e-6. ## 8 Analysis: what the stage-A log can and cannot say

**The stage-A log cannot separate the four features (E-11 can).** Stage A steps 1-40 ran without the four features and steps 41-400 with them. An earlier note said the KL slope "tripled" when the features began (0.00053 per step over steps 2-40, 0.00143 over steps 41-100). Reading the log: both numbers reproduce exactly, **but the factor is 2.0, not 3**: the first segment contains the learning-rate warm-up (steps 1-10); from step 11 the pre-feature slope is 0.00070 [0.00061, 0.00079] and 0.00143/0.00070 = 2.0. The KL was also already accelerating before step 41 (slopes over 15-step windows: 0.00048 for steps 11-25, 0.00101 for 26-40, 0.00158 for 41-55), the curve is smooth through step 41 with no kink, and it saturates by step ~90 (flat at 0.124 to 0.129 until step ~160). A power-law extrapolation of the pre-feature trend predicts a KL of 0.042 to 0.065 at step 100 against 0.131 observed, so the acceleration is faster than the extrapolated trend, but that is a statement about extrapolating 30 to 40 noisy points, not an identification of a cause. <!-- src: SPLIT sections 1-2; ledger R17 -->

**Nothing else accelerates at step 41.** The per-step slopes of the training reward (+0.0065 [0.0051, 0.0079] before, +0.0071 [0.0060, 0.0080] after), of the finished rate, of the greedy loop rate (-0.0020 before, -0.0011 after) and of the hard-fail share (-0.00138 before, -0.00040 after) are the same or steeper before step 41, because the failure rates were high and floor out. An interrupted-time-series fit finds level shifts at step 41 (KL +0.0086 [0.0021, 0.0149], reward +0.093 [0.041, 0.147], greedy loop rate -0.028 [-0.052, -0.004], hard-fail share -0.014 [-0.026, -0.003]), but the reward and the finished and loop rates are not the same quantity before and after step 41 (from step 41, 15% of the prompts carry the reference term and 25% of the groups contain the guide), and the shifts could equally be a resume artefact (checkpoint reload, RNG, a fresh prompt stream). They say "something changed at 41 on top of the trend", not which feature did it. The dev curve is evaluated only at steps 0, 50, ..., 400, so no dev point separates steps 1-40 from 41-50. <!-- src: SPLIT section 2; ledger R18 -->

## 12 Discussion

### 12.1 What the evidence supports

<!-- GATE E-01 --><!-- GATE E-03 --> The thesis we can defend is this: **supervised fine-tuning followed by RL with reference-free quality-estimation rewards beats the supervised model by a large margin on every benchmark we have; about half of the chrF++ margin (49%; 29% of the AfriCOMET margin) is finishing and looping that a one-line decode-time guard on the SFT also removes, so RL's contribution beyond a guarded SFT is +4.37 chrF++ and +0.048 AfriCOMET; most of the gain arrives in the first RL stage; later general RL and RL aimed at weak languages add little; against continued SFT for the same GPU time the registered verdict is inconclusive: RL matches the control on the 19 languages that had a teacher-translated corpus and is far ahead on the other 46 cells.** In detail:

1. *SFT plus RL beats SFT.* FLORES+ +8.81 [+8.59, +9.03], NTREX +7.91, MAFAND +5.01, all 93 cells +7.96 chrF++, held-out AfriCOMET +0.063; all 54 FLORES+ and 24 NTREX languages improve. This is a comparison of "SFT" against "SFT plus 8.66 A100-hours of RL"; the matched-compute control is item 9.
2. *About half of the chrF++ margin is finishing and looping, and a decode-time guard removes it.* Cutting the SFT's looping outputs at the loop onset recovers 49% of the chrF++ gain; 67 to 70% of the gain sits in rows where either model failed; about 41% of the AfriCOMET gain is finishing. The independent detector shows that the reward's own loop flag is a lower bound (1.77 times more flagged on mixed-best FLORES+), but the extra rows cost at most 0.19 chrF++ (3.0% to 4.4% of the gain), so the decomposition stands. What remains on well-formed outputs, +2.4 to +4.1 chrF++ and +0.037 AfriCOMET, is real and significant in 50 of 54 languages, but it is the part with the weakest control: the decode-time guard (E-01) shows the finishing share is a decoding fact, so RL's contribution beyond a guarded SFT is +4.37 [+4.28, +4.46] chrF++ and +0.0479 [+0.0469, +0.0490] AfriCOMET (+2.63 chrF++ [+2.56, +2.70] on rows where the guarded SFT is clean), with the caveat that the guard's best setting was at the edge of its grid. Beam-4 and MBR-16 at 768 tokens (E-02) give the same picture on a different intervention: the RL gain shrinks from +7.56 to +3.07 (beam-4) and +2.52 (MBR-16) chrF++ on 20 languages, and RL greedy stays +1.60 [+1.34, +1.88] ahead of the best decoded SFT. The matched-compute control (item 9) does not settle whether continued SFT could reach the same level on all languages: its registered verdict is inconclusive.
3. *Most of the rest arrives in stage A.* Stage A gives 94% of the gain to B1 on FLORES+ (7.96 of 8.50), including most of the finishing improvement; the dev curve gains +3.3 of +3.7 in 100 steps. We did not split the beyond-finishing part by stage.
4. *Later general RL and weak-group RL add little.* B1 adds +0.54 and the mixed stage +0.31 on FLORES+ (+0.30, +0.18 on NTREX, nothing on MAFAND); the continued RL on eight weak languages adds +0.03 [-0.06, +0.12] on its targets against a pre-registered +1.0. With one pair of extra seeds (E-04; difference 0.06 chrF++ [-0.02, +0.14] at 100 steps on 20 languages) the B1 step (+0.17 [+0.12, +0.21] on those languages) is about three times the seed difference, while **the mixed step (+0.07 [+0.02, +0.11]) is not separable from seed noise and we do not call it a gain**.
5. *Science SFT gives surface gains and no SSA-COMET-QE gain.* chrF +1.2, BLEU +1.5, spBLEU +2.0 against SSA-COMET-QE 0.6130 and 0.6118 (alpha 0 and 0.5), with no RL in the comparison. The science RL adds chrF +0.46 and AfriCOMET +0.005 at sentence level and nothing at document level (SSA-COMET-QE +0.004, chunked windows 0.574 to 0.577). E-14 shows the science SFT is needed: the same science RL started from the mixed best (no science SFT) ends -1.44 chrF [-1.61, -1.25] and -0.0082 AfriCOMET [-0.0101, -0.0065] below the executed science RL, and adds only +0.26 chrF [+0.12, +0.39] over its start, while the reward-family SSA-COMET-QE rates it level (-0.0006 [-0.0018, +0.0007]); one seed, one OOM restart, no `best/` checkpoint.
6. *Whole-document decoding fails and chunking is required.* Finished 0.71 and 0.51 (start and science SFT), loop 0.86 for both; chunked decoding finishes everything. *The first research's 270M RL-final is as good as or better at document level* (+0.019 window SSA-COMET-QE, +0.011 AfriCOMET window, +0.5 to +2.0 chrF). More generally, growth to 385M did not give a clear win over the first research: in the same harness, on rows clean for both generations, the 385M SFT is behind the old SFT on FLORES+, NTREX and MAFAND (-2.35 [-3.25, -1.38], -4.13 [-4.40, -3.85], -3.54 [-4.22, -2.75] chrF++); after RL the 385M model is level with the old RL on FLORES+ (+0.35 [+0.08, +0.62]) and NTREX (+0.02 [-0.07, +0.11]), 1.55 chrF++ below it on MAFAND [-1.72, -1.39], lower on held-out AfriCOMET on all three, and behind it at document level. 7. *Against NLLB.* The final model reaches 92% of NLLB-3.3B's chrF++ on 43 shared languages (3.26 below, [-4.29, -2.20] over languages) and closes 74% of the SFT's gap, with the largest relative standing in the lowest tertile; it matches or beats NLLB-3.3B on 7 of 43 languages. Under the caveats of Section 7.6 (reference set, beam against greedy, one seed) this is the most we say.
8. *The gain tracks how badly the SFT failed, not how much data a language has* (rho -0.47 with the SFT finished rate, rho -0.16 with tokens), with the fragility named in Section 7.7.
9. *Against continued SFT for the same GPU time (E-03).* The control (6,000 steps, 8.4 h, 50% replay + 50% NLLB-translated TranslatePsy pairs for 19 languages) reaches 29.97 chrF++ over the 93 cells against 34.55 for the RL (+4.58 [+4.45, +4.70] for RL; AfriCOMET +0.0183 [+0.0172, +0.0192]). The registered verdict is "inconclusive" because RL minus control on the 19 TranslatePsy languages is -0.03 [-0.13, +0.09] chrF++ (the condition is d > 0), while on the other 46 cells it is +9.29 [+9.09, +9.49]; the control repairs finishing and looping only on its 19 languages. One seed, one learning rate, one evaluated budget point (Section 7.2.2).

**Our reading.** These facts fit a simple account: the SFT model's main defect is unguarded greedy decoding (not stopping, looping), the hard-failure floor and the QE reward teach the policy to avoid it within about a hundred steps, and the translation-quality gain on top is modest. E-01 tested the first half of this account and supports it for chrF++ (a guard recovers 49%) and less for AfriCOMET (29%). E-03 adds that continued SFT on a teacher-translated corpus fixes finishing, looping and quality on the languages that corpus covers about as well as RL does, but does nothing for the languages it does not cover; what RL contributes beyond that, in our data, is breadth: one reward and the model's own samples improve all 54 FLORES+ languages. Whether a teacher corpus for more languages would close that breadth gap is not testable with the data we hold. The stage-A ablations (E-11) add that the four features of stage A are worth a small part of the gain (+0.32 chrF++ of +5.96 at 100 steps) and do not cause the KL rise.

### 12.2 What is not claimed

* That RL is in general a better, or more data-efficient, use of compute than more SFT: our one matched-compute control (one seed, one learning rate, one evaluated budget point, NLLB-teacher data for 19 languages) has the registered verdict "inconclusive" (R14; Section 7.2.2). <!-- GATE E-03 -->
* That the finishing gain is something only RL can deliver: a decode-time frequency penalty of 0.10 recovers 49% of the chrF++ gain (+3.89 of +7.96) and 29% of the AfriCOMET gain (+0.018 of +0.063) without training (E-01, `reports/e01_loop_guard_2026-11-04.md`).
* That the +2.4 to +4.1 chrF++ beyond finishing is "real translation quality" in a sense a human would confirm: there is no human evaluation, and the neural check (held-out AfriCOMET) is of the reward member's family.
* That the NLLB pseudo-reference or any reward component other than the stage-A features helped (not ablated); that the beam guide was worth its +25% step time (not separable from seed noise, E-11); that the four stage-A features cause the KL rise (they do not).
* That the mixed step (+0.31 on FLORES+) is a gain (not separable from seed noise on the 20 languages of the seed study), or that weak-group RL could not work with another design (one design, one seed).
* That the model is competitive with NLLB overall, or better on low-resource languages in general (Section 7.6); that it beats the first research's 270M model; that it improves science translation quality (SC3) or document translation (DL1, DL3).
* That NLLB-mined data caused the regression against the first research's SFT, or did not (K1; inconclusive).
* That the family, script and resource pattern is causal (Section 7.7).
* That the model is usable for Arabic dialects, Tamazight, Dinka, Tamasheq or Kanuri (chrF++ 2 to 14; English output rising).
* Any "first".

### 12.3 What would change the conclusions

E-01 outcome: the decode-time loop guard closes about half of the chrF++ gap and under a third of the AfriCOMET gap; RL plus guard minus SFT plus guard is +4.37 chrF++ [+4.28, +4.46] and +0.0479 AfriCOMET [+0.0469, +0.0490] (+2.63 chrF++ [+2.56, +2.70] on rows where the guarded SFT is clean), so the thesis stands in the form "RL's contribution beyond decoding is about +4.4 chrF++" (guard setting chosen on SFT dev from a pre-registered grid whose largest value won; a post-hoc dev-only extension to 0.2, 0.3 and 0.5 was still rising, and no full evaluation with a stronger penalty was run). Exceptions with the guard: FLORES+ zgh and MAFAND pcm are significantly worse for RL; on clean rows also aeb, ary, hau, ibo. One seed. E-03 outcome: the registered verdict of the matched-compute control is "inconclusive" (RL minus control +4.58 [+4.45, +4.70] chrF++ over 93 cells, -0.03 [-0.13, +0.09] on the 19 TranslatePsy languages, +9.29 [+9.09, +9.49] on the other 46 cells), so the paper claims neither that RL is a better use of the compute nor that it is not; the stage-A-matched and mixed-best-matched points were not evaluated. The mixed step is not separable from seed noise (E-04). The blocks of this paper that changed with E-01 and E-03 are listed in `PAPER_PLAN.md`, last section.

## 13 Limitations

1. **Single training seed** for every model and stage; all intervals are test-sentence bootstrap (1,000 resamples; Poisson weights shared across parallel languages for the RL-versus-SFT claims), and some are over languages only (NLLB, family analysis). Evaluation noise of the dev macro is sd 0.09 to 0.10 chrF++; training-run variation was measured only for the stage-A recipe at 100 steps on 20 languages with one pair of extra seeds (E-04: 0.06 chrF++ [-0.02, +0.14] between the two; Section 8); seeds of later stages, of the science and long-document stages and of the SFT are not available (a second SFT seed is impractical: stage B alone is 44.8 A100-hours). The mixed step (+0.31 on FLORES+; +0.07 on the 20 languages of the seed study) and per-language differences under about 0.5 chrF++ (pcm, hau, ibo) cannot be separated from seed noise.
<!-- GATE E-01 E-03 -->
2. **The matched-compute control is one run.** E-03 (Section 7.2.2) has one seed, one learning rate (the pre-registered probe's best of three), one data mix (50% replay + 50% TranslatePsy for 19 languages, with NLLB-3.3B targets) and **one evaluated budget point (6,000 steps)**: the stage-A-matched (3,100) and mixed-best-matched (4,800) checkpoints exist but were not evaluated, at the owner's request. Its registered verdict is "inconclusive" and was not changed. The decode-time loop-guard control (E-01) is reported in Section 7.2, with the caveats that its best setting was the grid edge and that a stronger penalty was explored only on dev.
3. **No human evaluation** (E-10: not run; it needs native-speaker annotators, which the owner cannot commission, and no LLM-judge substitute was validated). All evidence is automatic; the neural checks are of one family, and AfriCOMET has a length/entity bias (shown next to chrF++, never alone). One reference per sentence.
4. **Reward-family overlap.** Finished, loop and language-ID rates are optimised by construction; SSA-COMET-QE (the AfriScience-MT headline) is a checkpoint of the reward's family; chrF++ is partly optimised through the consensus utility and, for 5 + 7 languages, through the NLLB and human-reference terms. The reward's own loop flag undercounts degeneration by 1.77 on FLORES+ (Section 7.2.1), and the independent detector shares the repetition concept and is blind to non-repetitive failure.
5. **Ablations are limited** (E-11): three arms (no features, no reference domain, no beam guide) at 100 steps of the b64 recipe, one run per arm, sharing a 40-step prefix with seed 2, 20 languages, one pair of seeds as the noise yardstick; the KL-guard arm was not run (the guard never fired); dynamic sampling and the std floor alone, the reward components other than the reference term, and the later stages were not ablated; the mid-run changes of Section 14.5 mean the stages are not a clean single design.
6. **Benchmarks reused many times**: stage A, B1, mixed, weak-run and science evaluations all looked at FLORES+, NTREX and MAFAND; checkpoint choice used only the 720-sentence dev set (FLORES+ dev, 18 languages), but the "keep RL" decision rested on NTREX and MAFAND clean rows, and the weak-language choice used devtest-derived tables. No fresh, unseen evaluation set exists [E-21]. The post-hoc decomposition and the family analysis were run after the results were known; only the science analysis has rules fixed in advance.
7. **Contamination coverage.** Whole sentences of 3 or more words are absent from the audited text; 4 to 7-token phrases occur inside longer training text; paraphrase, MT-round-trip and Gemma-3 pretraining exposure are not covered; the packed-shard scans for AfriScience train overlap (0.9% of rows by target sentence in the A0 pair data) and for phrases were not done; the FLORES+ dev train half is in the SFT on purpose; stage A drew 6 flagged prompts of 28,120.
8. **Metric choices changed after the fact for the science analysis**: the headline moved from reference-based SSA-COMET-MTL to reference-free SSA-COMET-QE after a calibration on released sentence-level runs only; the paper's own checkpoint is unverified.
9. **Engine and decoding.** The bf16 engine parity gate was not met (9.6% identical outputs vLLM bf16 against HF bf16; chrF++ 36.22, 36.80, 38.18 on 240 sentences); E-09 shows the engines agree in fp32 (98.8% and 97.1% identical outputs for the SFT and the RL model), so the gate failure is bf16 rounding. E-09b: the SFT in fp32 on the full set differs from bf16 by -0.04 chrF++ [-0.13, +0.07] over 93 cells, so the bf16 headline does not overstate the RL gain; the earlier +0.24 to +1.81 range is superseded. Not done: the RL model in fp32 and AfriCOMET scoring of the fp32 SFT run. All headline numbers are vLLM 0.32.0 bf16 greedy at 768 tokens. Beam-4 and MBR-16 were measured at 768 tokens (E-02) on the SFT and the RL model only, on 20 languages x 200 sentences, with one SFT and one RL checkpoint, chrF++ as the MBR utility (metric-matched), a primary beam definition amended after a smoke test and before any score (stop at 4 ended hypotheses), and a second beam rule that gives a different sign for the RL model; the earlier 256-token beam numbers used a loop that ran past the end token and are not comparable.
10. **Language coverage.** 51 of 125 registry languages have no reference test and are unscored; many weak languages rest on one benchmark of 48 to 1,012 sentences; the family analysis covers 54 languages. The model fails on Arabic dialects, Tamazight, Dinka, Tamasheq and Kanuri, and RL increases English output there.
11. **Comparisons.** The old-versus-385M comparison (E-13, same harness, rows clean for both generations) is still a composite of data, steps, tokenizer layout and reward, with FLORES+ reduced to 4.7% of its rows; the NLLB comparison uses FLORES-200 published numbers against FLORES+, beam against greedy and language-level intervals; AfriqueLLM numbers come from a different few-shot harness; bidirectional aggregates are not compared.
12. **Documents.** 28 papers per language (168 documents; the document intervals of the long-document RL are bootstrap over documents and reflect paper sampling only; the other document tables have no bootstrap); whole-document decoding was tested at 4,096 new tokens and remains unusable after the long-document RL (72 to 73% loops); COMET encoders read 512 tokens, so only 4-sentence-window values are document scores; the 385M RL stages trained on prompts of at most 256 source tokens before the long-document RL.
13. **Cost and scale.** One 385M model and one language registry; A100 hours as in Section 5; no scaling claim.
14. **Unresolved and unevaluated items.** B1 final (step 135) was evaluated after the fact (E-20, `reports/e20_b1_final_2026-11-04.md`): it equals B1 best (step 50) within noise (+0.01 chrF++ [-0.02, +0.04], AfriCOMET +0.0004 over 93 cells; the 85 later steps changed nothing measurable), and the mixed stage is +0.26 [+0.22, +0.30] chrF++ over B1 final on FLORES+; the NLLB-data ablation was not run [E-17]; sentence-paired comparison with NLLB on the full devtest was not run [E-06b].
15. **Not run:** human evaluation (needs native-speaker annotators); the packed-shard contamination scans, the content check of A0 against g0, the embedding and MT round-trip paraphrase scans and the source of the other 34 dev-train-half languages (need the data box); the NLLB-data ablation (owner skipped); beam widths other than 4, length penalties and the 54-language decoding run; a science SFT at alpha 1.0 followed by RL, a second seed of E-14 and a long-document RL from the E-14 model.

### 14.6 Intended use and risks

Outputs are machine translations of unverified quality for languages without any human evaluation, with documented failure modes: Arabic dialects, Tamazight, Dinka, Tamasheq and Kanuri are not usable (chrF++ 2 to 14 after RL), RL increases English output there, and the model should be used sentence by sentence, not on whole documents. Religious text dominates the data of many low-resource languages, which may shift register and vocabulary toward it. The model must not be presented as a replacement for NLLB-class systems: it is 3.26 chrF++ below NLLB-3.3B on 43 shared languages. The owner's use case is production translation; the licences of NLLB and TranslatePsy prevent using those systems there, which is why we state exactly where NLLB-derived text and signals entered this model.
