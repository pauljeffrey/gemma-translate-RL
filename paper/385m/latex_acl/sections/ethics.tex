\textbf{Data and licences.} Part of the fine-tuning data is derived from NLLB. Sentences mined with NLLB make up about 14\% of the tokens of the main fine-tuning stage; adding NLLB-Seed and the Aya translations (NLLB-3.3B outputs) gives about 19\%, and adding machine-translated Tumbuka instructions (system not stated, possibly NLLB) about 23\%. NLLB models and outputs carry non-commercial (CC-BY-NC) terms, which limits commercial use of the NLLB-derived data and signals; a licence statement must accompany any release of the weights. Several sources have unspecified or unconfirmed licences (the \texttt{*\_mt560} OPUS sets, which are mostly Bible and Jehovah's Witnesses text and 69\% of the main pair data; \texttt{multi-open}; AfroLingu-MT; an Efik New Testament sheet) and need a legal decision. IbomMT is CC-BY-NC-4.0 and is a disclosed exception. Religious text dominates many low-resource languages. Reward-side data: NLLB-3.3B outputs (CC-BY-NC) served as a reward target for five languages only, and RL prompts (FineWeb-Edu, Wikipedia, news including BBC News, which declares no licence and carries BBC copyright) are input text only. The Gemma terms of use apply to the derived weights, and pre-training exposure cannot be excluded. Reward and monitor models, as declared on the Hub (read 2026-11-05): the two SSA-COMET and the two AfriCOMET checkpoints are apache-2.0; GlotLID declares \texttt{license: other} with \texttt{license\_name: apache-2.0-plus-notices}.

\textbf{The control's extra data.} For the SFT-only control we used TranslatePsy English-to-X pairs (CC-BY-NC-4.0; NLLB-3.3B translations of web sentences, filtered by AfriCOMET, SSA-COMET and MetricX) for that experiment only. No TranslatePsy row is in the SFT baseline, any RL stage, the science or long-document variants, or any released weight; the control checkpoint is neither released nor used in production. A good control result partly means ``distilling NLLB''. TranslatePsy-AfriSLM-0.8B and -2B appear only as comparison systems; they were distilled from NLLB-3.3B outputs. The NLLB-218 language-identification model (CC-BY-NC) was used for measurement only.

\textbf{Decontamination and its residuals (the clean 385M pipeline).} The audit found no benchmark sentence of three or more words in the audited training text. The remaining benchmark matches are one- or two-word strings, numerals and names (for example 0.25\% of NTREX rows and 0.47\% of MAFAND-MT rows). The FLORES+ development half (440 sentence ids) is in training on purpose, and the held-out half is protected. A lexical fuzzy scan of the pair and instruction files found no benchmark string of eight or more tokens as a contiguous run, but strings of four to seven tokens occur inside longer training text, and 263 benchmark strings of eight or more tokens overlap training text by character 5-grams; these are candidates, and we did not measure their effect. The first RL stage drew 6 flagged prompts of 28,120 from seven unfiltered prompt files, and 0.9\% of AfriScience-MT training rows have their African-language sentence in the earlier pair data (the test papers are untouched). We did not run paraphrase, machine-translation round-trip or packed-shard scans. These statements are about the clean 385M pipeline. Our earlier 270M pipeline trained on text that held the English side of 95\% of FLORES+ devtest; its numbers appear only on rows clean for both pipelines.

\textbf{Analysis decisions and process.} We chose checkpoints on a 720-sentence FLORES+ development subset, chose the languages for the weak-language run from devtest-derived tables, ran the decomposition and family analyses after seeing results, and changed the science headline metric after writing the interpolation rule. The authors overrode two failed training gates. The first RL stage was stopped after 40 steps and resumed with four added features; later stages were restarted once or twice, and the hacking alarm was loosened once. The science RL step cap was extended by the trainer, one long-document run lost most of its time to a disk-full crash loop, and the no-science-step run had one out-of-memory restart (replayed steps use the later records). LibreTexts rows were removed from the long-document pool before it started.

\textbf{Intended use, risks and dual use.} Outputs are machine translations of unverified quality, with documented failure modes: Arabic dialects, Tamazight, Dinka, Tamasheq and Kanuri are not usable, RL increases English output there, and documents should be translated sentence by sentence. The model should not replace NLLB-class systems or be used where mistranslation is costly (health, legal, safety). As with any translation model, it can produce text at scale in under-served languages, including spam or misleading content, and we release no content filter.
