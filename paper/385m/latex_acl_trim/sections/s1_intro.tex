\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% Findings: R1-R8, R19-R20, N2, W4, SC3, DL1-DL3, E01-E04, E11, E14.
Machine translation into African languages is hard for small models. Training text is scarce, and small models have little capacity to spare. Large systems such as NLLB-200 \citep{nllb2022nllb} help, but they are big, and the NLLB models and their outputs carry non-commercial licence terms.

Small models also fail in a characteristic way. After supervised fine-tuning (SFT), our model often does not finish its sentences: it stops midway, repeats a phrase until it runs out of space, writes in the wrong language, or copies the English input. Reinforcement learning (RL) with automatic quality scores as rewards is a natural remedy, and recent work reports gains for translation \citep{shao2024deepseekmath,feng2025mtr1zero,finkelstein2026translategemma}. Those gains, however, may come from something simpler. Repeating-and-not-stopping is partly a property of greedy decoding, which a decoding setting can change; and RL costs computing time that more SFT could use instead.

We therefore ask four questions about a 385-million-parameter English-to-African translator, trained first with SFT and then with RL:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1.} How much does RL improve translation, and for which languages?
\item \textbf{RQ2.} How much of the gain is finishing and not repeating, rather than better translation, and can a decoding change provide it?
\item \textbf{RQ3.} Does the same computing time spent on more SFT do as well?
\item \textbf{RQ4.} How reliable is the gain, and how does the model compare with a much larger system?
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Findings at a glance.}
\begin{itemize}
\item RL improves every language we test on three standard benchmarks (RQ1).
\item About half of the gain is finishing sentences and not repeating. A decoding setting recovers that half for the SFT model without training, but RL still adds clear gains beyond it (RQ2).
\item Decoding tricks such as beam search and minimum Bayes risk (MBR) decoding help the SFT model a lot and the RL model little (RQ2).
\item Matching the compute with more SFT is inconclusive by our pre-registered rule: it matches RL on the 19 languages where it had extra data, and is far behind on the others (RQ3).
\item Almost all of the gain arrives in the first RL stage. The model reaches about 92\% of the chrF++ of NLLB-3.3B on shared languages, at about one ninth of its size (RQ4).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) A controlled decomposition of an RL gain in small-model translation: a decoding control, a matched-compute control, seed and ablation runs, and an independent audit of the reward's own repetition check. (2) A decontaminated SFT and RL pipeline for 125 languages, with an audit of the training text. (3) Negative results: RL aimed at weak languages, science fine-tuning on a quality-estimation metric, and whole-document translation. Model artefacts: \anonreal{anonymised for review; released on acceptance}{model weights at \url{https://huggingface.co/AletheiaAING/gemma-0.4B-translate-rl} (see Ethical considerations)}.
We do \emph{not} claim that RL beats more fine-tuning in general, that our model beats NLLB, or any human-rated quality: all scoring is automatic.
