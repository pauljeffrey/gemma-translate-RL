\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}
% [paper.md 12.1-12.3; GATE E-01 E-03]
\textbf{What the evidence says.} RL improves a small translator on every benchmark we use, but the gain has two parts that deserve separate names. About half is finishing sentences and not repeating, which a decoding setting also gives the fine-tuned model; the other half is better translation that the setting does not provide. Almost all of it arrives in the first RL stage. More fine-tuning for the same time is a serious alternative where it has extra in-language data, and a much weaker one where it does not; our registered comparison is therefore inconclusive, and what RL adds in our data is breadth, since it improved every language from the model's own samples and a quality score. For science text, the fine-tuning step, not RL, carried the gain.

\textbf{What we do not claim.} We do not claim that RL is a better use of compute than SFT in general, that the improvement beyond finishing is quality a human would confirm, that our model beats NLLB or our earlier 270M model, or that any reward ingredient other than the four tested features helped. The model is not usable for Arabic dialects, Tamazight, Dinka, Tamasheq or Kanuri.

\textbf{What would settle the open points.} Evaluating the stronger repetition penalties and the two unevaluated control checkpoints, more seeds for the later stages, and human judgement of outputs. Together with the licence work in the Ethical considerations, these are the next steps.
