% Plain-language abstract. Numbers: gain of about nine chrF++ points on FLORES+ (+8.81, R1), 43 shared languages (N2), 92% of NLLB-3.3B (N2), about 8.6 times the parameters (nominal). Findings: R5/E01 (half), E03 (inconclusive), SC7/E14 (science SFT needed), E02 (decoding), E04 (seed pair).
Small language models translate many African languages poorly, and one common failure is that they do not finish their sentences: they stop midway or repeat themselves. We train a small English-to-African translation model with supervised fine-tuning, then with reinforcement learning that rewards outputs rated well by automatic quality scorers, and we ask what the second step adds. It improves every language we test, by about nine points of the standard chrF++ score on one benchmark. About half of that gain is finishing sentences and not repeating; a single decoding setting gives the fine-tuned model the same half without any extra training, while the rest of the gain remains. Spending the same computing time on more fine-tuning helps only the languages for which we supplied extra data, and by the rule we fixed beforehand the comparison is inconclusive. For scientific text, the extra fine-tuning step, not the reinforcement learning, carries the gain. On identical test sentences, our model stays about one point below NLLB-600M and about three below NLLB-3.3B (about 92\% of its score), which is about 8.6 times larger, and a model distilled from NLLB-3.3B is ahead of ours on its own languages. We trained one model per setting, checked seed noise on a subset only, and used automatic scores only; no person judged the outputs.
