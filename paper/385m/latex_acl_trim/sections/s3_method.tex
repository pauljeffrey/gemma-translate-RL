\section{Model, Data and RL Method}\label{sec:method}
% [M1-M11 L1-L4 CT1-CT3 R9 R10 W6]
\textbf{Model and tokenizer.} English to one of 125 registry languages with a fixed prompt and the answer \texttt{<tag> \{translation\}<end\_of\_turn>} in the Gemma 3 chat template; 74 languages have a reference test and are scored, RL covers 81. The 262,144-piece vocabulary is unchanged; each language has a single-token tag on an unused slot. The base is Gemma-3-270m-it \citep{gemmateam2025gemma3}; A0 adds six local-attention layers (UniMax-style schedule, 5,625 steps), and the 385M model adds 15 identity-initialised layers \citep[cf.][]{wu2024llamapro,kim2023solar}: 39 layers, hidden size 640, $385{,}144{,}448$ parameters.

\textbf{SFT.} Pairs and instruction data in 125 languages are filtered on both sides against a protected index of all benchmark dev, devtest and test splits (exact sentence, contained sentence, shared 8-gram; strings under 3 tokens exempt) and gated by AfriCOMET-QE. Stage B adds NLLB-mined bitext, the aligned part of Aya \citep{singh2024aya} and machine-translated Tumbuka instruction data (14.4\%, 18.7\%, 22.8\% of the 3.990\,B stage-B pack tokens, cumulatively). Muon \citep{liu2025muon} on matrices, AdamW elsewhere, 131,072 tokens per step, 32,853 steps (44.8 h for stage B on an A100), seed 1234; final validation loss 1.3412 (macro). \textbf{Decontamination and audit.} Beyond the intake filter, an independent audit scanned both sides of 25,911,095 training samples (A0 packs and the six stage-B shards) against all benchmark rows, with a positive control (the FLORES+ dev train half, in training on purpose, is found in 23,706 of 23,760 rows). It finds no benchmark sentence of three or more words in the audited text (FLORES+ devtest 0 of 54,648 rows); a lexical fuzzy scan of the pair and instruction files adds no benchmark string of 8 or more tokens as a contiguous run. The residual counts are listed once, in Ethical considerations.

\textbf{RL.} GRPO with groups of 16 completions (15 sampled plus the greedy output), group-standardised advantage and a k3 KL penalty (0.05) to the start model of each stage \citep{shao2024deepseekmath}; Muon, learning rate $2\times10^{-6}$, temperature 1.0, at most 384 new tokens (1,280 in b256), a KL guard that cuts the learning rate. Stage A runs 400 steps on English windows from FineWeb-Edu, Wikipedia and news (81 languages, 29 weakest up-weighted); B1 (135 steps, best checkpoint step 50 by dev \chrfpp{}) and a mixed stage (60 steps) follow, each from the previous checkpoint; the chain costs 8.66 \GPUh{}. The reward gives $-2$ for hard failures (wrong tag, empty, unfinished, copy of the source, repetition, wrong language by GlotLID, length) and otherwise a QE ensemble of \texttt{africomet-qe-stl-1.1} and \texttt{ssa-comet-mtl} without reference, shaped per language: the mean if $|r_1-r_2|\le1.0$, the minimum otherwise (42 languages); other languages use consensus \chrfpp{} among group members, and five weak languages add an NLLB-3.3B pseudo-reference term (weight 0.25, reward only) Soft penalties are never a bonus; as executed in stages A, B1 and mixed they are length (1.0 per log-unit outside the language's human length-ratio window, widened by 15\%), word ratio (0.5 per log-unit above the human 99th percentile), lexical (1.0 per unit of deficit in numbers, names and content words absent from the source), diacritics (1.0) and constraint anchors (boilerplate, copy, entity recall, function words, padding, repetition, a lexicon guard; a saturated stuffing deficit is a hard failure); the fluency and science-anchor penalties were off. Four features were added at step 41 of stage A: dynamic sampling, a standard-deviation floor, a beam/MBR-guided group member and a reference-anchored term for seven languages. A science SFT with weight interpolation, a science RL and a long-document RL follow (Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}).
