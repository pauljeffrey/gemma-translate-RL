## 2. Contamination and benchmark exposure

1. The 385M SFT text contains no benchmark sentence of 3 or more words (CONT, CT1). One- and two-word strings, numerals and names occur: NTREX 120 rows (0.25%), MAFAND 94 (0.47%), SmolSent 45 (0.12%), GlotStoryBook 15 (0.44%). The `clean` subset removes them; all tables agree with `all` within 0.01 chrF++ (CT2).
2. The FLORES+ dev **train half is trained on on purpose**; the held-out half is not. The RL dev set (720 sentences) is a FLORES+ dev subset; its language count is 16 or 18 depending on the report (X4). RL checkpoint choice used the dev set only. The weak-language choice used dev/devtest-derived per-language tables (POSTB1 "Weak-group continued RL: language choice" used FLORES+ devtest B1 chrF++ and held-out AfriCOMET to choose the 8 languages: **selection on a test set**, disclosed).
3. Benchmarks were evaluated many times (stage A, B1, mixed, weak run, science SFT alpha sweep on NTREX/MAFAND for the non-regression rule); the "keep RL" decision rested on NTREX and MAFAND clean rows (EVID 6.8). The alpha rule for the science SFT was written before the headline metric was fixed (SCI); the headline metric was later changed from SSA-COMET-MTL (reference-based) to SSA-COMET-QE after the calibration against released values (DOC). The post-hoc RL-vs-SFT decomposition (EVID) was run after the results were known; only the science analysis has pre-fixed rules.
4. The old 270M models (first research) saw the English side of 95% of FLORES+ devtest; their numbers appear only on their `clean` rows (n = 48 per language on FLORES+) and are descriptive. The first research's FLORES+ results were contaminated by its own pipeline (first paper Section 6.9a); this paper does not reuse them.
5. Phrase-level exposure found by the follow-up scan (ledger CT12): no benchmark string of 8 or more tokens occurs as a run in the reachable SFT pair text, but 4-7 token benchmark strings occur inside longer training text (MAFAND 134 of 17,428 English strings, GlotStoryBook 30, NTREX 18, SALT 12, FLORES+ devtest 0) and 263 (string, benchmark) cases of 8+ token English strings have character-5-gram Jaccard >= 0.5 with some training text (MAFAND 184; FLORES+ devtest 4 of 1,012); the audit's run rule tested such strings only for whole-sentence equality. Not covered by the audit or the follow-up: embedding / MT round-trip contamination; Gemma-3 pretraining; the RL prompt pools of the science and long-document stages (the b2048 science/general rows were scanned on 2026-11-03: 693 of 20,109 distinct science texts and 2,659 of 42,910 general texts flagged and dropped, LDOC); the NLLB pseudo-reference data; AfriScience train versus SFT sources; benchmarks not in the index (GATITOS, SmolDoc, SSA-MTE excluded earlier, AfriDocMT, AfriLongDoc, any benchmark version not on the A100).
6. FLORES+ and FLORES-200 references differ in places; the NLLB comparison uses FLORES-200 published numbers against FLORES+ devtest (N1).

## 3. Metric independence (state this table in the paper)

* **In the RL reward:** `africomet-qe-stl-1.1` and `ssa-comet-mtl` (reference-free; in the science RL also reference-based SSA-COMET-MTL), the consensus utility (chrF++ agreement among samples), a chrF++ term against human references on reference rows (weight 0.35; 7 languages), NLLB-3.3B pseudo-reference chrF++ (5 languages), the language-ID / copy / repetition / length / no-end-of-turn guards, and recall/precision/length/preservation terms in the science RL.
* **Therefore not independent for RL models:** finished rate, loop rate, LID pass (guards optimised by construction); any QE score (AfriCOMET-QE, SSA-COMET-MTL, and SSA-COMET-QE, the AfriScience-MT paper's headline, a different checkpoint of the reward's family); chrF++ on the 12 languages with a chrF++ or pseudo-reference reward term (the AfriScience TEST references are never used).
* **Independent evidence:** chrF, chrF++, spBLEU, BLEU against the human references of FLORES+ devtest, NTREX, MAFAND, SmolSent, SALT, GlotStoryBook, AfriScience-MT dev/test; held-out reference-based `masakhane/africomet-stl-1.1` (never a reward; the RL's monitor and hacking alarm; same family and training data as the QE reward member, so independent in weights and in use of the reference, not in family).
* Checkpoint selection by RL stages used the dev chrF++ (and, from the mixed stage, the held-out AfriCOMET as a veto and in the combined stop). The long-document RL selects by its own hold-out reward (the optimised quantity).
* AfriCOMET has a length/entity bias (`reports/data/africomet_bias_2026-11-02/`); it is shown next to chrF++, never alone. SSA-COMET-QE reproduces the AfriScience-MT paper's released values with mean absolute difference 0.006 on the six strongest released runs and 0.031 on nine (DOC): the paper's own checkpoint is unverified.

## 5. Statistical and methodological caveats

* **One training seed per model and stage**; all intervals are 95% paired sentence-bootstrap over test sentences (1,000 resamples). Two bootstrap schemes appear (equal language weight; Poisson weights shared across parallel languages): X5. The dev macro's evaluation noise is sd 0.09 to 0.10 chrF++; training-run variation is unmeasured. The +0.54 and +0.31 steps, and per-language differences under 1 chrF++ (pcm, hau, ibo), cannot be separated from seed noise.
* **No matched-compute SFT control, no decode-time loop guard, no human evaluation** (until E-01, E-03, E-10 are done); **no ablation of any RL feature**; the NLLB-data ablation was skipped.
* **Language sets differ between macros** (X7); 50 of 125 registry languages have no reference test and are not scored; many weak languages rest on a single benchmark of 48-1,012 sentences; one reference per sentence.
* **Subsets** (clean-row, loop-free, cut-at-loop) are survivor-biased and the cut-at-loop rule is a heuristic (R5).
* **Greedy 768-token decoding in vLLM 0.32.0 bf16** is the headline; the engine parity gate was not met (9.6% identical outputs vLLM bf16 vs HF bf16); vLLM fp32 vs HF fp32 not run (D6). Beam/MBR results apply to the SFT model, 200 sentences x 20 languages, 256-token cap, chrF++ utility (metric-matched).
* **The old-vs-385M comparison** is a composite (data, steps, tokenizer, code state); old numbers have no intervals.
* **Documents:** 28 papers per language, no bootstrap; COMET encoders read 512 tokens, so concatenated-document COMET is not a document score; the honest document scores are 4-sentence-window values; the 385M RL stages never trained on prompts above 256 source tokens (before the long-document RL).
* **External comparisons** are indicative: different prompts, shots and harnesses (AfriqueLLM, AfroBench); TranslatePsy and AfriNLLB aggregates are bidirectional and are not compared; published NLLB Yoruba chrF on AfriScience-MT looks anomalous (N6).
* **Dev/test hygiene:** `masakhane/africomet-stl-1.1` is used for monitoring and in the hacking alarm and combined stop of the mixed stage and later, i.e. it influenced early stopping; it was never a reward.

## 6. Ethics and use

Release of weights and data must follow the licence audit above (CC-BY-NC components, NLLB-derived signals, unresolved MT560, unspecified-licence sources); outputs of the model are machine translations of unverified quality for languages where no human evaluation exists, with failure modes documented (Arabic dialects, Tamazight, Dinka, Tamasheq are not usable: chrF++ 2 to 14 after RL, loops in 11% to 56% of FLORES+ outputs, W3). The owner's use case is production translation; the licence of NLLB and TranslatePsy prevents using those systems there, which is the motivation to state exactly where NLLB-derived text and signals entered this model. TranslatePsy data appears in this paper only as the training data of the research control of ledger section 12 (E-03), and in no released model.
